# Plan

1. Load ERSSTv5 monthly `sst` with `load_regular_data`, 1950-01 to 2023-12, on the native 2-degree grid, cut to the Nino 3.4 box 5S-5N, 170W-120W (a single contiguous box, no dateline crossing).
2. Reduce the box to one monthly regional series with `compute_basic_stat`, `op='area_weighted_mean'` over latitude and longitude, weighting only the cells that are valid in the field.
   - Note [omip-weighted-means-and-integrals]: the 5S-5N, 170W-120W mean must be area-weighted, using `compute_basic_stat` with `op='area_weighted_mean'` and weights restricted to the same NaN-aware valid cells as the data; an unweighted latitude mean would be wrong.
3. Build the two reconstructions from that single regional series with `compute_anomaly_vs_own_climatology`, once with the 1991-2020 monthly climatology (RECON_9120, 1950-01 to 2023-12) and once with the 1981-2010 monthly climatology (RECON_8110, same months).
   - Note [anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices]: each anomaly is defined only against its own baseline period, so RECON_9120 and RECON_8110 differ by the offset between the two climatologies, not by any change in the underlying data; keep both on their stated baselines and never mix them.
4. Load the published series with `load_regular_data` from `cpc_indices`: product `ersst5_91-20`, Nino 3.4 anomaly (the reference), product `ersst5_81-10`, Nino 3.4 anomaly (the control), and product `oni` (the official 3-month running index), monthly, covering 1950-2023 as far as each series extends.
5. Correlate RECON_9120 with the CPC 1991-2020 Nino 3.4 anomaly over 1950-2023 using `analyze_correlation`, reporting the Pearson correlation to 6 decimal places.
   - Note [serial-correlation-and-significance]: correlate the two series as they are, without `detrend=True`, because they are two estimates of the same quantity and their shared trend is part of the agreement being checked; report correlation, RMSE and max deviation rather than p-values.
6. Score RECON_9120 against the CPC 1991-2020 series with `compute_difference_metrics`, reporting the RMSE to 4 decimal places and the largest absolute departure (its absolute value to 4 decimal places, with the year and month at which it occurs).
7. Form the 3-month centred running mean of RECON_9120, of RECON_8110 and of the CPC `oni` series with `temporal_aggregate` (running window of 3 months), keeping the full 1950-2023 record except where the running mean is undefined at the ends.
8. Apply the operational ONI criterion with `detect_threshold_events` to the 3-month running-mean series: episodes with values at or above +0.5 degC lasting at least 5 consecutive overlapping 3-month seasons (El Nino), and episodes at or below -0.5 degC lasting at least 5 seasons (La Nina). Run it for RECON_9120 and for CPC `oni`; from each El Nino list take the count over 1950-2023 and the three episodes with the highest peak, each with the year and month of its peak and the peak value to 2 decimal places. Retain the La Nina lists for the shading in Figure 1.
9. Compute the reconstructed baseline offset as the unweighted time mean of RECON_8110 minus RECON_9120 over 1950-01 to 2020-12 with `compute_basic_stat` (single time series, so no spatial weighting is involved), reported in degrees Celsius to 3 decimal places.
10. Compute the published baseline offset the same way from the CPC series, product `ersst5_81-10` Nino 3.4 anomaly minus product `ersst5_91-20` Nino 3.4 anomaly, unweighted mean over 1950-01 to 2020-12, to 3 decimal places, and compare it with the Step 9 value.
11. Figure 1 (own plotting code): two rows sharing a 1950-2023 time axis. Upper row: RECON_9120 and CPC 1991-2020 monthly anomalies, their 3-month running means, the +0.5 and -0.5 degC thresholds, and shading between the threshold and the running-mean curve - warm for the El Nino episodes and cool for the La Nina episodes from Step 8. Lower row: RECON_9120 minus CPC on its own vertical scale. Both vertical axes labelled in degrees Celsius.
12. Figure 2 (own plotting code): square scatter of RECON_9120 (y) against CPC 1991-2020 (x), one point per month, identical axis ranges and ticks, a 1:1 reference line, and the correlation and RMSE from Steps 5-6 annotated.

Other notes:
- The reconstruction is on a fixed 1991-2020 monthly climatology, whereas the published ONI uses a 30-year base period that is updated every 5 years; applying the identical threshold-and-duration criterion to each therefore yields different event counts, and the difference is an artifact of the baseline, not of the underlying SST field.
- The baseline offset is evaluated on 1950-01 to 2020-12 only, because the 1981-2010 control series ends in 2020, so the comparison window is shorter than the 1950-2023 window used for the correlation and event statistics.
- Peak values and peak months refer to the 3-month running-mean series and are reported at the month in which that running mean peaks.
