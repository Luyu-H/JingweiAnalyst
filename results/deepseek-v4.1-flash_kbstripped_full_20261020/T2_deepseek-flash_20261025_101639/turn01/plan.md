# Plan

1. Load ERSSTv5 monthly `sst` with `load_regular_data` for 1950-01 to 2023-12 over 5S-5N, 170W-120W (lat -5 to 5, lon 190E-240E), on the native 2-degree grid.
2. Build RECON_9120: apply `compute_anomaly_vs_own_climatology` to the loaded SST subset using its own 1991-2020 per-calendar-month climatology, then reduce over latitude and longitude with `compute_basic_stat` using `op='area_weighted_mean'` to obtain the monthly Nino 3.4 anomaly series for 1950-01 to 2023-12.
   - Note [omip-weighted-means-and-integrals]: the 5S-5N, 170W-120W mean must be computed with `compute_basic_stat` and `op='area_weighted_mean'`; a plain mean is wrong on a latitude-longitude grid.
   - Note [anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices]: build RECON_9120 and RECON_8110 as anomalies against ERSSTv5's own monthly climatology for each calendar month (1991-2020 and 1981-2010 respectively), not against WOA23; for the event step, count a phase only where the series stays beyond the threshold for `min_duration=5` consecutive 3-month steps, not by eye.
3. Build RECON_8110 identically from the same ERSSTv5 subset but with the 1981-2010 per-calendar-month climatology, area-weighted over 5S-5N, 170W-120W, giving a monthly series for 1950-01 to 2023-12.
4. Load the CPC published series with `load_regular_data`: product `ersst5_91-20`, Nino 3.4 anomaly variable (main reference); product `ersst5_81-10`, Nino 3.4 anomaly variable (control); and product `oni`, the official 3-month running ONI series, all for 1950-01 to 2023-12.
5. Apply `temporal_aggregate` with a 3-month centred running window to RECON_9120 and to the CPC `ersst5_91-20` Nino 3.4 anomaly for event detection and Figure 1. Do not smooth the ONI series again: it is already the published 3-month running index.
6. Run `detect_threshold_events` with threshold +0.5 degC and `min_duration=5` consecutive 3-month steps on the smoothed RECON_9120 series to identify El Nino events, and with threshold -0.5 degC and `min_duration=5` to identify La Nina events for Figure 1 shading. Run the identical +0.5 degC, `min_duration=5` detection on the CPC ONI series. For each series, record the event count, each event's duration, peak value and the year/month of the peak; sort the El Nino events by peak value and take the three highest for both RECON_9120 and ONI.
7. Compute `analyze_correlation` between RECON_9120 and the CPC `ersst5_91-20` Nino 3.4 anomaly over 1950-01 to 2023-12, with no detrending, and report the Pearson correlation to 6 decimal places.
   - Note [serial-correlation-and-significance]: correlate RECON_9120 and the CPC series as they are (`detrend=False`), since their shared trend is part of the agreement; if a significance value is quoted, use the effective sample size derived from the lag-one autocorrelation instead of the number of months.
8. Compute `compute_difference_metrics` between RECON_9120 and the CPC `ersst5_91-20` Nino 3.4 anomaly over 1950-01 to 2023-12: report RMSE in degC to 4 decimal places and the largest absolute deviation in degC to 4 decimal places with its year and month.
9. Compute the reconstructed baseline offset by forming RECON_8110 minus RECON_9120 month by month and taking the unweighted time mean over 1950-01 to 2020-12 with `compute_basic_stat` (`op='mean'`, time dimension, no time weighting); report it in degC to 3 decimal places. Compute the published offset the same way from CPC `ersst5_81-10` minus `ersst5_91-20` Nino 3.4 anomaly over the same months, report it to 3 decimal places, and compare the two offsets.
10. Produce Figure 1 as a two-row plot sharing the 1950-2023 time axis: upper row overlays RECON_9120, the CPC 1991-2020 anomaly, their 3-month running means, the +0.5/-0.5 degC thresholds, and warm/cool shading between the threshold and the running-mean curve during the detected El Nino/La Nina events; lower row plots RECON_9120 minus CPC on its own vertical scale. Label both vertical axes in degC.
11. Produce Figure 2 as a square scatter of RECON_9120 (y) against the CPC 1991-2020 Nino 3.4 anomaly (x), one point per month over 1950-2023, with identical axis ranges and ticks, a 1:1 reference line, and the correlation and RMSE annotated.

Other notes:
- The RECON_8110-minus-RECON_9120 offset is expected to differ from zero by a constant per calendar month because the two reconstructions use different base periods; this is a baseline artefact, not an error, and the baselines used must be stated.
- ONI is already a 3-month running index and is not smoothed a second time; RECON_9120 and the CPC `ersst5_91-20` anomaly are smoothed with a 3-month centred running mean only where the 3-month criterion or the running-mean overlay requires it.
- The RECON_9120 and ONI El Nino counts can differ because the threshold and 5-season persistence test are applied to two different anomaly constructions: a fixed 1991-2020-baseline area-weighted ERSSTv5 reconstruction versus CPC's operational ONI series; near-threshold events can therefore gain or lose the required five consecutive seasons.
- Carry all series unrounded through the calculations and round only the reported statistics, to the decimal places requested.
