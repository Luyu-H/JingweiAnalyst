The reconstruction was built from ERSSTv5 monthly SST (`dataset="ersst"`, `variable="sst"`, `field="total"`) for 1950-01 to 2023-12 over latitude -5 to 5 and longitude -170 to -120, on the native 2° grid. Monthly anomalies were computed with `compute_anomaly_vs_own_climatology` using `freq="month"`, `method="subtract"`, and base periods 1991-01 to 2020-12 for RECON_9120 and 1981-01 to 2010-12 for RECON_8110. The regional mean was then formed with `compute_basic_stat(op="area_weighted_mean", dim=["lat","lon"])`. CPC series were loaded with `load_regular_data(dataset="cpc_indices", variable="NINO3.4", field="anomaly")` for products `ersst5_91-20`, `ersst5_81-10`, and `oni` over 1950-01 to 2023-12. RECON_9120 and CPC 91-20 were aligned on a common time axis. Three-month centred running means were computed only for RECON_9120 and CPC 91-20 with `temporal_aggregate(..., freq="rolling", window=3, center=True, op="mean")`; ONI was not smoothed again. Event detection used `detect_threshold_events` with +0.5°C (`direction="above"`, `min_duration=5`) and −0.5°C (`direction="below"`, `min_duration=5`). Correlation used `analyze_correlation(..., method="pearson", detrend=False)`. Difference metrics used `compute_difference_metrics`. Baseline offsets were unweighted time means over 1950-01 to 2020-12 using `compute_basic_stat(op="mean", dim="time")`.

Key results:
- RECON_9120 versus CPC 1991-2020 Nino 3.4 anomaly, 1950-01 to 2023-12: Pearson r = 0.999972 (6 d.p.; no detrending; effective sample size ≈ 47.4). RMSE = 0.0075°C (4 d.p.). Largest absolute deviation = 0.0226°C (4 d.p.) at 1992-01.
- RECON_9120 El Niño count under the operational ONI-style criterion (3-month centred running mean ≥ +0.5°C for at least 5 consecutive 3-month steps): 16 events. Top three by peak: 2015-12, peak 2.64°C, duration 14 steps; 1997-12, peak 2.35°C, duration 12 steps; 1983-01, peak 2.15°C, duration 11 steps.
- CPC ONI El Niño count under the identical criterion: 22 events. Top three by peak: 2015-12, peak 2.59°C; 1997-12, peak 2.37°C; 1983-01, peak 2.14°C. The counts differ because the threshold and five-season persistence test are applied to two different anomaly constructions: an area-weighted ERSSTv5 reconstruction on a fixed 1991-2020 monthly climatology versus CPC’s operational ONI series. Near-threshold events can therefore gain or lose the required fifth consecutive season.
- Reconstructed baseline offset, mean of RECON_8110 − RECON_9120 over 1950-01 to 2020-12: 0.060°C (3 d.p.). Published CPC offset, mean of CPC 1981-2010 − CPC 1991-2020 over the same months: 0.058°C (3 d.p.). The reconstructed minus published offset is 0.0018°C ≈ 0.002°C, so the two offsets agree very closely.
- From the same detection run, RECON_9120 detected 25 La Niña events and ONI detected 20; these were used for the cool shading in Figure 1.

Figure 1 is a two-row figure sharing the 1950-2023 time axis. The upper row overlays the monthly RECON_9120 and CPC 1991-2020 anomalies, their 3-month centred running means, the ±0.5°C thresholds, and shading between the threshold and the running-mean curve during detected RECON El Niño (warm) and La Niña (cool) events. The lower row shows RECON_9120 minus CPC 1991-2020 on its own vertical scale. Both vertical axes are labeled in °C. Figure 2 is a square monthly scatter of RECON_9120 (y) against CPC 1991-2020 Nino 3.4 anomaly (x) over 1950-2023, with identical axis ranges and ticks, a 1:1 reference line, and the correlation and RMSE annotated.

The main insight is that the independently reconstructed Nino 3.4 series is almost indistinguishable from the CPC 1991-2020 series, with sub-hundredth-degree RMSE and a maximum monthly discrepancy of only about 0.023°C. The El Niño count difference is therefore not a reconstruction failure but a consequence of applying the ONI persistence criterion to a different anomaly/baseline construction, especially for near-threshold events. The reconstructed 1981-2010 versus 1991-2020 baseline offset also reproduces the published CPC offset to within about 0.002°C.
