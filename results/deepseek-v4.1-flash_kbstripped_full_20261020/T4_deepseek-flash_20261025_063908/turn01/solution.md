**Direct answer.** Using each model’s own monthly `thetao`, `so`, `mlotst` and `areacello` over 1971-01 to 2000-12, the offline mixed-layer-depth operator does **not exactly reproduce** the published `mlotst`, but it is closely correlated with it and has the expected systematic sign: in all six model–month cases the mean difference (operator MLD minus `mlotst`) is negative, meaning the operator MLD is shallower than `mlotst`. Internal consistency is therefore high in pattern, but with a systematic shallow bias and localized large disagreements.

**Methodology as executed.** For CESM2, GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR, `load_regular_data` was called for `cmip6_historical` variables `thetao`, `so`, `mlotst` over `["1971-01","2000-12"]`, and static `areacello` without a time range. Vertical levels were loaded with `load_depth_levels` for `thetao`; the shallowest level centre, `levels[0]`, was used as `ref_depth`. The operator MLD was computed with `compute_mixed_layer_depth(temp_data=thetao, sal_data=so, ref_depth=levels[0], method='density', density_threshold=0.03, temperature_type='potential')`. Thus the diagnostic was applied to each monthly profile first, and the monthly MLD fields were then averaged with `temporal_aggregate(freq='climatology', op='mean')`; no climatological mean T/S profile was used to compute an MLD. March and September climatologies were selected separately for the operator MLD and for published `mlotst`.

For each model and month, `build_comparison_mask`-equivalent logic was implemented directly: a cell was retained only if both the operator MLD and `mlotst` were non-missing in all 30 instances of that calendar month. The same mask was applied to both climatologies before all statistics. Mean difference and RMSE were computed with `compute_difference_metrics` using `weighting='area'`; the spatial Pearson correlation was computed with `analyze_correlation` over `['lat','lon']` on the masked climatologies. The exceedance percentage was computed with `compute_exceedance_fraction` on `|operator − mlotst|`, threshold 20 m, `comparison='abs_greater'`, `weighting='area'`. Coverage was computed as the area of the common mask, using the model’s own `areacello`, divided by that model’s total ocean area from `areacello.sum()`. No WOA23 land–sea mask was used in the comparison; WOA23 was used only as the regular display grid for Figure 1 via `regrid_horizontal(..., target='woa23', method='conservative')`.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CESM2 | March | -4.5 | 7.5 | 0.998 | 2.7 | 96.4 |
| CESM2 | September | -2.6 | 5.1 | 0.998 | 0.2 | 97.9 |
| GFDL-ESM4 | March | -9.6 | 21.4 | 0.942 | 6.3 | 97.2 |
| GFDL-ESM4 | September | -8.0 | 14.3 | 0.985 | 7.5 | 98.5 |
| MPI-ESM1-2-LR | March | -2.5 | 6.8 | 0.994 | 1.5 | 96.8 |

The common mask covers more than 96% of each model’s total ocean area in all six cases, so the statistics are not dominated by masking. The negative mean differences range from about -2.4 m to -9.6 m. RMSE ranges from 5.1 m to 21.4 m. The exceedance fraction is smallest for CESM2 in September (0.2%) and largest for GFDL-ESM4 in September (7.5%) and March (6.3%).

**Ranking of internal consistency.** Using area-weighted RMSE against `mlotst` as the primary criterion, with the spatial correlation and mean difference reported alongside:

- **March, most to least internally consistent:** MPI-ESM1-2-LR (RMSE 6.8 m, corr 0.994, bias -2.5 m), CESM2 (RMSE 7.5 m, corr 0.998, bias -4.5 m), GFDL-ESM4 (RMSE 21.4 m, corr 0.942, bias -9.6 m).
- **September, most to least internally consistent:** CESM2 (RMSE 5.1 m, corr 0.998, bias -2.6 m), GFDL-ESM4 (RMSE 14.3 m, corr 0.985, bias -8.0 m), MPI-ESM1-2-LR (RMSE 14.3 m, corr 0.969, bias -2.4 m). The unrounded September RMSEs for GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR are very close (14.277 m vs 14.316 m), so their ranking by RMSE is effectively a near-tie; GFDL-ESM4 has the slightly lower RMSE and higher correlation, while MPI-ESM1-2-LR has the smaller mean bias.

This is a ranking of the internal consistency of the diagnostic, not a ranking of model quality.

**Interpretation and expected causes of disagreement.** Both quantities come from the same simulation, so every disagreement is a property of the diagnostic, its vertical discretization, and the exact definition of `mlotst`, not evidence of model physics error. The operator and `mlotst` are not expected to agree exactly because the operator used the model’s shallowest level as `ref_depth` rather than a standard reference level; it used the `method='density'` formulation with a 0.03 kg m⁻³ density threshold and `temperature_type='potential'`; and it interpolated the crossing between bracketing vertical levels. In addition, the diagnostic was applied to each monthly profile and the MLDs were averaged afterward, i.e. the diagnostics were computed before the climatological averaging, never from a 30-year mean T/S profile. The vertical grid near the base of deep mixed layers adds further discretization error. The largest absolute departures reported by the code occur at high latitudes, including North Atlantic and Southern Ocean locations (for example, CESM2 March near 60°N, 58°W; CESM2 September near 75°S, 55°W; MPI-ESM1-2-LR March near 54°N, 51°W; MPI-ESM1-2-LR September near 72°S, 137°W), consistent with the expected concentration in deep mixed-layer/deep-convection regions and the Arctic.
