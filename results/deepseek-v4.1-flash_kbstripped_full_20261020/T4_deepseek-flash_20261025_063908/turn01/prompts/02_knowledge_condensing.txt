### [cmip6-mlotst-definition] How CMIP6 mlotst is defined, and why an offline MLD will not match it exactly
(type: concept; severity: must)
#### Key points
- CMOR: `mlotst` = "Ocean Mixed Layer Thickness Defined by Sigma T", standard
  name `ocean_mixed_layer_thickness_defined_by_sigma_t`, comment "Sigma T is
  potential density referenced to ocean surface", cell_methods `time: mean`.
  `mlotstmax` and `mlotstmin` are the monthly maximum and minimum of
  instantaneous MLD. [CMOR Omon; OMIP-phys A1]
- The OMIP criterion follows Levitus (1982). A **surface parcel** (S and Θ of
  level k = 1) is displaced down to level k **at that level's in-situ pressure
  p(k)**. Its density is compared with the local density at p(k):
  - ρ_displaced = ρ[S(1), Θ(1), p(k)] and ρ_local = ρ[S(k), Θ(k), p(k)]
  - δB = −g (ρ_displaced − ρ_local) / ρ_local
  - The MLD is the first depth where δB > ΔB_crit = 0.0003 m s-2, linearly
    interpolated between that depth and the one above it. [OMIP-phys H24.2,
    eqs. H39–H42]
- With g = 9.8 m s-2 and ρ ≈ 1035 kg m-3, ΔB_crit corresponds to
  **Δρ_crit = 0.03 kg m-3**, the de Boyer Montégut et al. (2004) value. Some
  studies use 0.125 kg m-3, which gives deeper mixed layers. The choice "should be
  compatible across models and observations". [OMIP-phys H24.2]
- The MLD asked for is **not** the KPP boundary-layer depth, and not the bulk
  mixed layer of isopycnal models. [OMIP-phys H24.2]
- OMIP recommends computing `mlotst` **online, every model time step**, "to
  avoid aliasing". The monthly value is the time mean of those instantaneous
  depths. [OMIP-phys H24.2, A1]
- Observational MLD climatologies often use a 10 m reference depth. The GFDL paper,
  for example, evaluates its model against WOA13 with "the 0.03 density threshold
  relative to the upper 10 m". [GFDL §4.9]
#### Implications for this project
- To compare an offline MLD with a model's own `mlotst`:
  - pass `ref_depth` equal to the model's shallowest level to
    `compute_mixed_layer_depth`, since `mlotst` is referenced to the ocean
    surface, not to 10 m. That removes the largest definitional difference,
    but not all of it: the result comes close to `mlotst`, never identical.
  - compute the MLD of each monthly profile and then average the monthly MLDs.
    Never average temperature and salinity first: the MLD of an averaged
    profile is not the average of the MLDs and is systematically shallower,
    because averaging smooths the pycnocline away.
  - leave out the columns that never reach the threshold (below) and report how
    much area that excludes.
- An MLD recomputed offline from monthly-mean `thetao`/`so` differs from the
  model's own `mlotst` for three reasons, even with the same 0.03 threshold:
  1. The reference level: OMIP uses the model's top level, while the default
     `ref_depth` of `compute_mixed_layer_depth` is 10 m.
  2. The density form: OMIP uses a displaced-parcel comparison at local pressure,
     the offline operator a σ0 difference.
  3. Time averaging: OMIP averages instantaneous MLDs, the offline operator uses
     the MLD of averaged profiles.
  State these differences when comparing the two. Do not treat the residual
  as model error.
- Because of point 3, an offline MLD from monthly means is expected to come
  out **shallower** than `mlotst`, even with the same threshold and reference
  level. A monthly mean is itself an averaged profile.
- **Columns where the threshold is never reached** are encoded differently
  in the three models' `mlotst`. This was checked on March 1990 against cells
  where the offline operator finds no crossing: GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR
  report roughly the full column depth (median `mlotst/deptho` 0.96 and 0.85),
  while CESM2 reports 0 m in 1077 of 2486 such cells, mostly on Arctic
  shelves. Exclude these columns from any operator-versus-`mlotst` comparison.
  No single fill value treats all three models fairly.
- GFDL-ESM4 computes `mlotst` on its native 75 hybrid layers. The archived
  `thetao`/`so` it would be compared with were remapped to 35 z levels, which
  gives that model an extra resolution difference.
- The defaults of `compute_mixed_layer_depth` (0.03 kg m-3 from 10 m) are the
  de Boyer Montégut criterion that observational MLD climatologies use, so an
  MLD computed with them is directly comparable with those climatologies.
  Comparisons with SEANOE (0.03 kg m-3, 5 m or 10 m reference; see
  `configs/datasets.yaml`) mix criterion and reference-depth differences with
  real bias. Say which ones apply.
#### Common mistakes
- Calling `mlotst` a "0.03 kg m-3 from 10 m" MLD.
- Comparing a 0.125 kg m-3 MLD with `mlotst`.

### [monthly-means-are-time-averages] CMIP6 monthly fields are time averages of every model time step, so nonlinear diagnostics from them are not the model's own
(type: pitfall; severity: must)
#### Key points
- Each requested period (day, month, year) "is sampled as a time average. Time
  averages include all model time steps over the given period; there is no
  sub-sampling. Products of time-dependent fields are time-averaged as a
  product". [OMIP-phys A1]
- `mlotstmax`/`mlotstmin` are the extremes over snapshots within the month.
  Adjacent points "need not have extrema registered at the same time step".
  [OMIP-phys A1, H24.1]
- Squared fields (e.g. `tossq`, `mlotstsq`) are accumulated online so that
  variance can be computed. Squaring a monthly mean does not give them.
  [OMIP-phys Table H1 caption, H25]
- MLD is recommended online "in order to avoid aliasing". [OMIP-phys H24.2]
- For extensive quantities, "experience at GFDL with Boussinesq z* models
  suggests that errors are minor when working offline using time averaged
  thickness and time averaged tracer concentration". Errors are "generally
  unacceptable" for isopycnal models. [OMIP-phys F3]
#### Implications for this project
- f(monthly mean) ≠ monthly mean of f for any nonlinear f. Examples are density
  from T and S, MLD by a threshold crossing, depth of a maximum gradient, a
  threshold exceedance (hypoxia) or a ratio of two tracers. The order of
  operations therefore changes the answer and must be chosen on purpose:
  - **diagnostic first, then average** reproduces the mean of the diagnostic
    and matches how archived online diagnostics are defined;
  - **average first, then diagnostic** gives the diagnostic of the mean state.
    This is the like-for-like choice when the reference exists only as a
    climatology.
  State which order was used and why.
- Monthly variance within a month cannot be recovered from monthly means. Only
  interannual or inter-month variance can.
- Averaging monthly fields over a period needs day weights in the model's own
  calendar. `temporal_aggregate` does this, and equal weights do not: calendar
  months are 28 to 31 days long, so an equal-weight average of monthly means is
  not a time average. `compute_basic_stat(op='mean', dim='time')` gives every
  month the same weight. `temporal_aggregate` is the only tool that weights by
  month length and so the only correct way to average monthly data over a
  period that spans different calendar months; its `freq='mean'` gives the
  mean state of a period, the field a bias or an RMSE is measured on. Day
  weights do not enter a monthly climatology, each calendar month averaged
  across the years of a baseline (what an anomaly or a climate index is taken
  against): that is the plain mean of that month's values over the years, as
  climate normals are defined, and `compute_anomaly_vs_own_climatology` and the
  index tools build it so.
#### Common mistakes
- Calling an MLD from climatological T/S profiles "the model's climatological
  MLD". It is a different quantity from the time mean of the monthly MLDs.
- Reporting a variance of monthly means as if it were sub-monthly variability.

### [common-comparison-mask] One comparison sample: a shared mask for model–observation and multi-model metrics
(type: method; severity: must)
#### Key points
- Regridding model output to a spherical grid "generally involves a land–sea
  mask distinct from the model native mask". [OMIP-phys A3] After remapping,
  each source covers its own set of cells, and coastal cells differ most.
- The GLODAPv2 mapped climatology is a global 1° × 1° product on 33 standard
  depth surfaces, mapped from the GLODAPv2 bottle data. [Lauvset et al. 2016]
  It shares the horizontal geometry of the WOA 1° grid, but not WOA's data
  coverage or its land-sea mask.
#### Checked in the local files
- GLODAPv2.2016b surface `TCO2` has 41320 valid cells on its 1° grid, and
  WOA23 `landsea_01` calls 1459 of them land: 442 north of 60°N, 179 south of
  60°S, the rest coastal cells at all latitudes. `build_comparison_mask` on the
  GLODAP field keeps 39861 cells with `ocean_only=True` and all 41320 with
  `ocean_only=False` (checked 2026-10-20).
#### Implications for this project
- A model is comparable with an observation, or with another model, only where
  both have data, and several models can be ranked against each other on a
  cell-by-cell score (a bias, an RMSE or the pattern correlation of one field)
  only where all of them do. Left to itself each pair covers a different set of
  cells, so such a score from one pair describes a different piece of ocean
  from the next, and a ranking between them means nothing. Put the fields on
  one grid, build one mask over all of them with `build_comparison_mask`, and
  apply it with `.where(mask)` to every field before any metric is taken.
- Where the shared mask does not apply: a pattern that is itself the result of
  an analysis on each source's own domain, such as the regression pattern of a
  variability mode (see `eof-modes-and-pdo-index`). Each mode was computed on
  its own source's cells, so masking the patterns afterwards to the other
  sources' coverage does not make them comparable. Correlate each with the
  reference pattern over the cells the two share (`analyze_correlation` leaves
  out the cells either one lacks), and compare the models on those
  correlations. Nor does a mask built on one level carry to another: coverage
  shrinks with depth, so a statistic at several depths takes its cells from the
  fields at each depth, not from the surface mask.
- The numbers of one cell-by-cell comparison come from one sample. Report the
  means that `compute_difference_metrics` returns over the shared sample
  (`mean_data`, `mean_reference`) together with the bias, not means computed
  separately over different cells. A global or regional mean of each source
  over its own ocean, with its own weights, is a different quantity (see
  `omip-native-grid-vs-regridded`): when the difference of such means is asked
  for, take each mean on its own grid and say that the difference is not the
  bias on the shared mask.
- `ocean_only=True`, the default of `build_comparison_mask`, is right only when
  WOA23 is part of the comparison. The check is made on the grid, so a
  GLODAP-against-models comparison on the shared 1° geometry silently inherits
  WOA23's coastline and drops cells where GLODAP has data and WOA23 says land.
  Those are among the hardest cells for a model to match, so dropping them
  flatters every model. Pass `ocean_only=False` when the reference is not WOA23.
- Report the cell counts the mask records alongside the metrics. A mask much
  smaller than the individual fields means the products disagree about where
  the ocean is, not that the models are poor.
#### Common mistakes
- Scoring each model on its own pairwise mask in a cell-by-cell comparison of
  one field, and then ranking the models on those scores.
- Applying the WOA23 land-sea mask to a comparison against GLODAP.

### [cmip6-grid-labels] CMIP6 grid labels gn / gr and what they imply for the three local models
(type: dataset; severity: should)
#### Key points
- Grid labels [CVs grid_label]:
  - `gn`: "data reported on a model's native grid"
  - `gr`: "regridded data reported on the data provider's preferred target grid"
  - `gr1`, `gr2`, …: other regridded grids. `gnz`: zonal mean on the native
    latitude grid. `gm`: global mean.
- OMIP: structured-grid models should archive on the native grid. The same
  variable on native and spherical grids keeps the same CMOR name, and the
  two "can never coexist in the same file". GFDL described its plan to coarsen
  ocean output to a 1° spherical grid to reduce archive size. [OMIP-phys A3.3–A3.4]
- Models with a moving vertical coordinate (z\*, isopycnal, hybrid) have
  **time-dependent cell thickness and volume**. [OMIP-phys B1.2]
#### Checked in the local files
`grid_label` and `grid` global attributes of `thetao`, read on 2026-09-24:

| model | grid_label | grid attribute | nominal_resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| CESM2 | gn | "native gx1v7 displaced pole grid (384x320 latxlon)" | 100 km |
| GFDL-ESM4 | **gr** | "ocean data regridded from tripolar - nominal 0.5 deg latitude/longitude to 180,360; interpolation method: conserve_order1" | 1x1 degree |
| MPI-ESM1-2-LR | gn | "gn" | 250 km |
#### Implications for this project
- GFDL-ESM4 values are already remapped, so "native-grid" numbers for GFDL
  are numbers on its 1° archive grid. The land–sea mask is the regridded one,
  and budgets are only approximately conservative. The native hybrid layers
  (75) were remapped to 35 z levels, which is why `volcello` is monthly for
  GFDL-ESM4.
- Resolution differs by a factor of about 2.5 between CESM2 (100 km) and MPI
  (250 km). Features narrower than the coarse grid (boundary currents,
  equatorial undercurrent, coastal upwelling) are not comparable in amplitude.
- Within one model, variables can sit at different staggered points on the
  same gn grid (see `velocity-components-are-grid-relative`).

### [omip-native-grid-vs-regridded] When to work on the native grid and when regridding is acceptable
(type: method; severity: should)
#### Key points
- Two aims conflict: spherical-grid output is easy to map and difference,
  while native-grid output preserves conservation for budgets. "regridding …
  generally involves a land–sea mask distinct from the model native mask. When
  changing the land–sea mask it is difficult to ensure conservation even when
  using 'conservative' regridding methods." [OMIP-phys A3]
- The loss of conservation "may be of minor consequence for many metric-based
  analysis purposes. However, it sacrifices the needs of budget analyses."
  Regridding "merely for visualization purposes and/or to produce difference
  maps" can ignore non-conservation. [OMIP-phys A3, A3.2]
- Structured-grid models archive on their native grid. The standard spherical
  targets are the 1° or ¼° grids of the World Ocean Atlas. [OMIP-phys A3.3]
- In the vertical, models on z, z\*, p or p\* need no regridding. Coarsening to
  the 33 WOA standard levels is allowed, but "care should be given to models
  that do not share the same 5500 m depth as the World Ocean Atlas".
  [OMIP-phys A4, C2]
- For transports across sections (overturning, passages, heat transport) on
  non-spherical grids, compute along a native-grid "zig-zag" path that
  approximates the section rather than regridding fluxes. [OMIP-phys C4]
#### Implications for this project
- A good pattern for model-versus-observation work: compute every **number**
  (global and basin means, inventories, integrals) on each source's native grid
  with its own weights. Regrid to the WOA 1° grid only for **maps, pattern
  correlation and RMSE on a common mask**. Say which numbers come from which
  grid. The exception is an analysis whose result depends on the grid and is
  compared between sources, such as an EOF mode with its explained variance
  and its regression pattern (see `eof-modes-and-pdo-index`): put every
  source, the observations included, on one common grid before the analysis,
  and take its numbers there.
- After regridding a field for a cell-by-cell comparison, build one shared
  valid-data mask for all sources before computing pattern statistics. Coastal
  cells otherwise differ between the sources (see `common-comparison-mask`,
  which also says when a pair's own shared cells are the sample instead, as
  for the patterns of a variability mode).
- Regrid only for the cell-by-cell comparison. An average, a trend or an
  integral belongs on the field's own grid with its own cell areas, because
  regridding replaces those with the target's.
- Method in `regrid_horizontal`: 'conservative' is the right choice going to a
  coarser grid and before any area-weighted step; 'bilinear' is the fallback
  when a model's corner mesh is unusable; 'nearest_s2d' is for masks and other
  categorical fields. A regridded RMSE or pattern correlation depends on the
  method, so state the method whenever one is quoted.
- Vertical interpolation onto another level set changes layer-integrated
  quantities. Integrate thickness-weighted quantities (inventories, OMZ
  thickness) on the original levels.
- Values at fixed depths (a bias "at 100, 500 and 1000 m") need each product
  interpolated to those depths (`regrid_vertical`), not the nearest level
  selected: vertical grids differ between products and rarely line up, so each
  product would answer at a different depth, and the products differ most
  where the gradients are steepest. This interpolation is for comparison, not
  for budgets.
- Diagnostics located at a vertical extremum, such as a nutricline (the depth of
  the maximum vertical gradient), are quantised by the vertical grid. Where the
  profile is smooth, in the subtropical gyres especially, the maximum can jump
  between levels, so read such a map together with the gradient itself.
#### Common mistakes
- Computing a global inventory after regridding to 1° and comparing it with
  the native-grid value as if they should agree.
- Regridding velocity components as scalars (see
  `velocity-components-are-grid-relative`).

### [cmip6-static-grid-fields] Meaning of the CMIP6 ocean grid fields areacello, volcello, deptho, sftof (and the absent thkcello/masscello)
(type: concept; severity: should)
#### Key points
- **areacello**: "Horizontal area of ocean grid cells" (m2, cell_methods
  `area: sum`). This is the area of a **tracer** cell, and it "should be zero or
  missing over land". All CMIP6 ocean models have fixed horizontal grids, so
  the area is static. [CMOR Ofx; OMIP-phys B, G7]
- **volcello**: "Ocean Grid-Cell Volume" (m3, `area: sum`). This is an
  extensive field. It exists as a fixed field (Ofx) and as a time-varying one
  (Omon). [CMOR Ofx, Omon]
- **deptho**: "sea floor depth for present day relative to z=0 geoid.
  Reported as missing for land grid cells." It is the bottom "from the
  perspective of the model tracer fields". [CMOR Ofx; OMIP-phys G5]
- **sftof**: "Percentage of horizontal area occupied by ocean" (%). [CMOR Ofx]
- **thkcello** (cell thickness, m) and **masscello** (mass per area, kg m-2)
  are part of the request. For models with static cells,
  masscello = rhozero × thkcello, where rhozero is the model's Boussinesq
  reference density. That density is not the same in all models: OMIP
  suggests 1035 kg m-3, and early GFDL models used 1000 kg m-3. Where cell
  thickness varies in time, both are monthly fields. [CMOR Ofx/Omon; OMIP-phys
  B1–B2, G3]
- For full cells, thickness can be taken as the difference of the layer
  depth bounds. That fails at partial top and bottom cells. [OMIP-phys B1.1]
#### Implications for this project
- None of the three models' local archive has `thkcello`, `masscello` or `rhozero`.
  The layer thickness that respects partial bottom cells is
  **volcello / areacello**. The `lev_bnds` difference is right only for full
  cells. Checked on the local historical files (January 2000, cells where
  `thetao` is valid):
  - **GFDL-ESM4**: area × layer-bound thickness gives 1.53e18 m3 against
    1.33e18 m3 from `volcello`, and 14 % of cells differ by more than 1 %.
    Its remapped z levels carry values in bottom cells that are only partly
    water.
  - **MPI-ESM1-2-LR**: partial bottom steps differ by up to ±50 % in 3.3 % of
    cells.
  - **CESM2**: the two agree within 0.6 %.
  `cell_thickness_like` in `src/tools/data_loader.py` returns volcello /
  areacello for CMIP6 fields. `identify_oxygen_minimum_zone` and the depth
  averages use it.
- `deptho` is model bathymetry, not true bathymetry. Deciding "is this level
  above the model sea floor" should use `deptho` or the fill pattern of the
  field itself, not an external topography.
- GFDL-ESM4's `volcello` varies in time because its native vertical coordinate
  moves (see `cmip6-grid-labels` and `configs/datasets.yaml`). The loaders
  already handle the time axis.
- On a curvilinear grid the model's own `areacello` is the only correct area
  weight, and the published `volcello` alone accounts for the partial cells at
  the sea floor. `load_areas` and `load_volumes_for_cmip6` use them where the
  model publishes them and record the origin in `attrs['area_source']` and
  `attrs['volume_source']`, which is worth reporting when they feed an
  integral.
- Total ocean area differs between models by a per cent or so, because a
  coarser grid resolves the coastline differently. That is a property of the
  model, not an error to correct: state the area each integral was taken over
  rather than rescaling one model to match another.

### [cmip6-thetao-potential-temperature] CMIP6 thetao is potential temperature referenced to the sea surface
(type: concept; severity: should)
#### Key points
- The CMIP6 diagnostic protocol requires every model to archive **potential
  temperature θ**, `thetao`, referenced to the ocean surface. This holds whatever
  the model's prognostic temperature is. [OMIP-phys D2, H10]
- Models whose prognostic variable is TEOS-10 **Conservative Temperature Θ**
  still convert it online to θ for `thetao`. They archive Θ separately as
  `bigthetao`. The CMOR comment on `bigthetao` reads: "this should be contributed only
  for models using conservative temperature as prognostic field". [OMIP-phys
  D2, H11; CMOR Omon]
- CMOR standard_name of `thetao`: `sea_water_potential_temperature`, units
  `degC`, cell_methods `area: mean where sea time: mean`. The CMOR comment
  says: "Diagnostic should be contributed even for models using conservative
  temperature as prognostic field."
- Potential temperature is referenced to 0 dbar, so **surface θ equals surface
  in-situ temperature**. SST (`tos`) is also potential temperature, not
  Conservative Temperature. [OMIP-phys H13]
#### Implications for this project
- None of the three local models provides `bigthetao`. Any TEOS-10 calculation
  (density, N², mixed layer) must treat `thetao` as potential temperature and
  convert it with the potential-temperature route, not the in-situ route.
  `temperature_kind()` in `src/tools/scientific_calculator.py` already reads this
  from the metadata. Taking the wrong route biases the density increasingly
  with depth.
- Which density for which purpose: a sigma referenced to a fixed pressure
  (sigma0 at the surface, sigma2 or sigma4 for the deep ocean) is what labels a
  water mass, because it removes the compression that makes any water denser
  with depth. The in-situ density, at each cell's own pressure
  (`compute_potential_density_anomaly` with `ref_pressure=None`), is what
  mass-per-volume arithmetic needs: converting a concentration between molar
  bases, or a volume into a mass (see `cmip6-bgc-units-per-volume`).
- Comparing `thetao` with an **in-situ** temperature climatology (WOA23 and
  GLODAP report in-situ temperature) is exact at the surface. The two
  quantities diverge with depth. For surface-only comparisons (SST maps,
  top-level bias) no conversion is needed. For sub-surface comparisons one side
  must be converted.
#### Common mistakes
- Treating `thetao` as Conservative Temperature because the model is "modern".
  None of the three local models archived Θ.
- Converting SST from potential to in-situ temperature. The two are identical
  at 0 dbar.

### [cmip6-so-practical-salinity] CMIP6 so is (an approximation of) Practical Salinity, units "0.001"
(type: concept; severity: should)
#### Key points
- Most CMIP6 ocean models use pre-TEOS-10 thermodynamics. Their prognostic
  salinity approximates **Practical Salinity S_P** (PSS-78), the quantity
  stored in observational databases such as WOA. [OMIP-phys D4]
- The protocol states that CMIP6 salinity comparisons are made against Practical
  Salinity whatever the model thermodynamics. This matches earlier CMIPs.
  [OMIP-phys D4]
- TEOS-10 recommends **Absolute Salinity S_A** for thermodynamic
  calculations. S_A reflects the mass of dissolved constituents, while S_P
  depends only on conductivity. Adding non-ionic silicate raises S_A and density
  but leaves S_P unchanged. [OMIP-phys D4]
- CMOR units for `so`, `sos` and `sob` are the string **`0.001`**, meaning parts per
  thousand (dimensionless). The CMOR comment warns that the unqualified term
  "salinity" "does not necessarily imply any particular method of
  calculation". [CMOR Omon]
- Salt content in a cell is formally cell mass × S_A. Pre-TEOS-10 models,
  whose conserved salinity is S_P, report salt content as cell mass × S_P.
  [OMIP-phys D5]
#### Implications for this project
- Model `so` and WOA23 salinity (practical salinity, units "1") can be
  differenced directly. No unit conversion is needed: "0.001", "1e-3", "1" and
  "psu" all denote the same practical-salinity number.
- For density or other TEOS-10 routines (gsw), convert S_P to S_A with the
  location-dependent function before calling routines that expect S_A.
  Passing `so` straight in as S_A biases density slightly, and the error varies
  by region.
- A unit parser that rejects "0.001" is not a sign of bad data. Map it to
  practical salinity explicitly.
#### Common mistakes
- Multiplying `so` by 0.001 or 1000 because of the units string. The stored
  numbers are already of order 35.
- Calling a salinity difference "psu" in a report while treating the field as
  Absolute Salinity in a density calculation.

[cmip6-mlotst-definition]
- Operator MLD: displaced surface parcel compared at each level's in-situ pressure, δB = −g(ρ_displaced − ρ_local)/ρ_local, first depth where δB > 0.0003 m s-2 (Δρ_crit = 0.03 kg m-3), linearly interpolated between bracketing levels; pass `ref_depth` = the model's shallowest level, not 10 m.
- Compute the MLD of each monthly profile and average the MLDs (never average `thetao`/`so` first); expect the offline MLD to be shallower than `mlotst`; exclude columns that never reach the threshold and report the area excluded; state the three definitional differences (reference level, σ0-difference vs displaced parcel, averaging order) and do not treat the residual as model error.

[monthly-means-are-time-averages]
- f(monthly mean) ≠ mean of f: the MLD of an averaged profile is not the average of MLDs — diagnostic first, then average; state the order used.
- The March/September climatology is the plain mean of that month's values across the 30 years; day weights do not enter a single-calendar-month climatology.

[common-comparison-mask]
