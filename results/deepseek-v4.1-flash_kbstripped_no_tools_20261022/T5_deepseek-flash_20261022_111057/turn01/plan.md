# Plan

1. **Load the data.** Open `cmip6_historical` (`so`, `areacello`, `volcello`) for CESM2, GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR over 1950-01 to 2014-12; `cmip6_ssp245` (`so`, `areacello`, `volcello`) for the same three models over 2071-01 to 2100-12; and `woa23` (`salinity`, 1971-2000 annual climate normal, `landsea_01`, `basinmask_01`). Keep each model on its native horizontal grid and retain the level coordinate and cell bounds/thicknesses for the 0-10 m selection.
   - Note [historical-ssp245-member-continuity]: CESM2 historical is r1i1p1f1 while its ssp245 is r10i1p1f1 (historical r10 is not archived), so CESM2 delta SSS and slope ratio include an inter-member internal-variability difference; GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR are r1/r1 and continuous. Do not fit a trend across the 2014/2015 join; the historical and ssp245 trends are fitted separately within their own segments.

2. **Compute SSS at every cell, for every model month and for the WOA23 climatology.** For each cell, select the levels whose depths lie within 0-10 m (WOA23: 0, 5 and 10 m; each model: its one or two levels in that range) and form `SSS = Σ(so × volcello) / Σ(volcello)` over those levels, using each level's full cell volume and no partial-layer or overlap weighting. For WOA23 derive the full cell volume from its 1° cell area and the level thickness implied by the depth bounds. For models use the supplied `volcello`; for WOA23 use its grid-cell volume. Keep values in psu. Form annual means per cell (mean of the 12 monthly fields in each year) for the model time series.
   - Note [cmip6-so-practical-salinity]: model `so` (units `0.001`) and WOA23 salinity are both practical-salinity numbers of order 35; difference them directly with no ×0.001/×1000 factor and report in psu, and do not pass them as Absolute Salinity into gsw routines.
   - Note [omip-native-grid-vs-regridded]: compute global and basin means, trends and delta SSS on each model's own grid with its own `areacello`/`volcello` weights; regrid only for the bias maps, RMSE and pattern correlation, using conservative horizontal regridding, and state the method whenever a regridded RMSE or correlation is quoted. Follow the question's SSS definition (full-cell-volume-weighted mean of levels within 0-10 m, identically for models and WOA23, no vertical interpolation to a common depth), which differs from interpolating each product to a fixed depth.

3. **Mean-state comparison against WOA23.** Form each model's 1971-2000 SSS climatology (mean of monthly SSS over 1971-2000), regrid it conservatively to the WOA23 1° grid, and build one comparison mask over all sources (three models + WOA23, `ocean_only=True`); apply `.where(mask)` to every field. On the shared cells compute the bias (model minus WOA23), RMSE and spatial Pearson correlation per model, and report the mask cell counts. Report each model's and WOA23's mean SSS and the bias from the shared sample, not from means computed on each source's own cells, and quote the regridding method with the RMSE and correlation.
   - Note [common-comparison-mask]: put every field on the WOA23 1° grid, build one mask over all sources and apply `.where(mask)` before bias, RMSE and pattern correlation so all three models are scored and ranked on the same cells; report the mask's cell counts (a mask much smaller than the fields means the products disagree about where the ocean is). Take `mean_data` and `mean_reference` from the difference metrics over the shared sample and quote them with the bias.
   - Note [cmip6-grid-labels]: GFDL-ESM4 is `gr`, its "native" fields are already remapped to 1° with the regridded land-sea mask, so its weights are 1° archive weights; CESM2 (100 km) and MPI (250 km) are `gn` and differ ~2.5× in resolution, so features narrower than the coarse grid are not comparable in amplitude.

4. **Historical trend, 1950-2014.** On each model's native grid compute the annual global mean SSS series (area-weighted with `areacello`) for 1950-2014, then fit a linear trend in psu per decade with the effective-sample-size correction, method `linear`, and report the slope, its 95 % confidence interval, R², and whether the interval excludes zero. Also compute the cell-by-cell linear trend of annual-mean SSS over 1950-2014 with `method='linear'` and the effective-sample-size correction, and identify cells significant at p < 0.05 for stippling; regrid the trend field to a regular longitude-latitude grid for display only.
   - Note [serial-correlation-and-significance]: fit the slopes with `method='linear'`, quote the effective-sample-size correction (applied by default; corrected values quoted), give the slope with its 95 % interval and call it significant only if the interval excludes zero; on the trend map draw the slope at every cell and stipple cells significant at p < 0.05; for the spatial correlation with WOA23 read the coefficient, not its p-value.

5. **ssp245 trend, 2071-2100.** Repeat the annual global-mean fit of step 4 on the ssp245 2071-2100 segment only, using [serial-correlation-and-significance] as in step 4, and report slope, 95 % interval, R² and significance. Compute the ratio of the ssp245 slope to the historical slope per model, reported to 1 decimal place, and state whether the ratio is meaningful.

6. **End-of-century change, delta SSS.** For each model compute the ssp245 2071-2100 mean SSS minus the historical 1995-2014 mean SSS on its native grid, weighted with `areacello`/`volcello` as in steps 2-4, and report the global mean delta and the Atlantic, Indian and Pacific basin means. Carry the WOA23 `basinmask_01` onto each model grid, use the surface labels through the whole water column (`depth_dependent=False`), fold marginal seas into their parent ocean (`include_marginal_seas=True`), and exclude the Southern and Arctic Oceans, which are separate basins; report per-basin cell counts.
   - Note [basin-and-region-definitions]: basin statistics use the WOA23 basin mask carried onto each grid with `include_marginal_seas=True`; say the mask is a WOA labelling, not the OMIP 10-region one, and that the Southern and Arctic Oceans are separate basins; use the surface labels applied through the whole water column (`depth_dependent=False`) for named-ocean means, and check the per-basin cell counts because marginal seas in the WOA23 mask may have no model counterpart.

7. **Figures.** Figure 1: three global maps in one row of the SSS bias from step 3 on the shared comparison cells, one diverging colour scale centred on zero shared by all panels, colour bar labelled in psu. Figure 2: three global maps in one row of the cell-by-cell 1950-2014 trend from step 4 in psu per decade, each on a true geographic longitude-latitude frame (native curvilinear fields regridded to a regular grid for display only), one shared diverging colour scale centred on zero, with p < 0.05 cells stippled on top. Figure 3: one time-series panel of the global mean SSS of all three models, historical 1950-2014 and ssp245 2071-2100 drawn as separate segments with the 2015-2070 gap left empty, each segment overlaid with its own fitted line from steps 4-5, y axis labelled in psu, and each model in a colour used consistently across all three figures.

Other notes:
- The ratio of the ssp245 to the historical slope compares two different periods (30 vs 65 years) and different forcing levels; it is a descriptive ratio only, and for CESM2 it also mixes realisations, so it is not a like-for-like measure of forced amplification and the answer must say so.
- The headline mean-state means and biases are quoted on the common comparison sample; native-grid areacello-weighted means are used for the trend and delta statistics, so small differences between the two sets of means are expected.
