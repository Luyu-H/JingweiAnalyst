### [cmip6-carbonate-system-definitions] CMIP6 dissic, talk and ph: what they contain and on which pH scale
(type: concept; severity: must)
#### Key points
- **dissic**: "Dissolved inorganic carbon (CO3+HCO3+H2CO3) concentration",
  mol m-3. It is C_T = CO2* + HCO3- + CO3--. [CMOR Omon; OMIP-BGC §2.6]
- **talk**: "total alkalinity equivalent concentration (including carbonate,
  nitrogen, silicate, and borate components)", mol m-3. OMIP requires
  A_T = A_C + A_B + A_W + A_P + A_Si (+ others). It notes that neglecting the
  phosphate and silicate terms "has been common among model groups but leads to
  systematic errors in computed pCO2". [CMOR Omon; OMIP-BGC Eq. 32]
- **ph**: standard name `sea_water_ph_reported_on_total_scale`. The comment
  is "negative log of hydrogen ion concentration with the concentration
  expressed as mol H kg-1". OMIP asks for the **total pH scale** and the
  Dickson et al. (2007) best-practice constants. Derived quantities are
  computed through the whole water column (in situ), not only at the surface.
  [CMOR Omon; OMIP-BGC §2, §2.6]
- Model implementations:
  - GFDL-ESM4 (COBALTv2) uses **mocsy 2.0** with Lee et al. (2010) total boron,
    Millero (2010) K1/K2 and Dickson & Riley (1979) Kf. [GFDL §2.6]
  - MPI-ESM1.2 (HAMOCC6) moved to the total scale and the Dickson constants, and
    added phosphate and silicate alkalinity. [MPI §5.2]
  - CESM2 (MARBL) has "fully prognostic carbonate chemistry". The paper does
    not name the constants. [CESM2 §2.2]
#### Implications for this project
- Compare model `ph` only with an observational pH on the **total scale at
  in-situ temperature and pressure**. A pH at 25 °C / 0 dbar is a different
  quantity. Among GLODAP's pH fields, check which one is in-situ
  (`configs/datasets.yaml` lists `pHtsinsitutp` and `pHts25p0`).
- `dissic` and `talk` are per volume. See `cmip6-bgc-units-per-volume` for the
  conversion to µmol kg-1.
- The CESM2 paper does not document its carbonate constants. Mention this as
  an unverified assumption if the comparison depends on them.

### [cmip6-bgc-units-per-volume] CMIP6 biogeochemical tracers are per unit volume (mol m-3, kg m-3)
(type: concept; severity: must)
#### Key points
- In the OMIP tracer equation "conservation of volume is assumed … and
  standard units of mol m−3 are used for all tracers". [OMIP-BGC §2.4]
- The CMOR comments define "mole concentration" as "number of moles per unit
  volume, also called 'molarity'". [CMOR Omon, o2/no3/po4/si]
- Units as archived [CMOR Omon; OMIP-BGC Tables 6, 10, 12]:

  | variable | CMOR units | common reporting unit | factor (volume basis) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | o2, o2sat, no3, po4, si, dissic, dissoc, talk, phycos | mol m-3 | mmol m-3 = µmol L-1 | × 1e3 |
  | dfe | mol m-3 | nmol L-1 (nM) | × 1e6 |
  | chl | kg m-3 | mg m-3 (≈ µg L-1) | × 1e6 |
  | ph | 1 | – | – |

  The factors in the last column are unit arithmetic. They change the prefix
  but keep the volume basis.
#### Implications for this project
- Numbers in mmol m-3 are **still per volume**. WOA23 and GLODAP report
  µmol kg-1 (per mass). Changing the prefix does not make the two comparable.
  Only a density conversion does: µmol kg-1 = mmol m-3 × 1000 / ρ, where ρ is in
  kg m-3. `configs/datasets.yaml` (`cross_dataset_notes.units`) records this
  and notes that no loader converts automatically.
- The density error only cancels in quantities that are invariant under scaling:
  ratios of two tracers converted with the same ρ, or depths of extrema and
  gradients. Absolute concentrations, inventories against observations and
  thresholds on a mass basis all need the conversion. The scale-invariant
  quantities need no conversion at all and are best taken from the native
  field: a constant ρ cancels exactly, while the in-situ density, which grows
  with depth, rescales each level by a different factor and so itself shifts
  a depth located from the shape of a profile (a nutricline, the depth of a
  maximum gradient) by a little.
- Inventories (mol) are volume integrals of the per-volume field: Σ c · volcello.
  No density is needed for these on the model side.
- Two concentrations on different bases cannot be compared, differenced or
  evaluated against each other until one has been converted. The conversion
  needs a density, and the one that mass-per-volume arithmetic needs is the
  in-situ density, `compute_potential_density_anomaly` with `ref_pressure=None`
  (a sigma at a fixed reference pressure is not). `transform_unit` defaults to a
  constant density, the mean surface value, which errs by about a per cent in
  the deep ocean and in fresh polar water, enough to rival the differences a
  model-observation comparison sets out to measure. Pass the in-situ density
  field for anything below the surface that needs converting (not for the
  scale-invariant quantities above), and state which density was used (the
  result's `attrs['density_assumption']`).
- For inventories prefer the volume basis: a model's native mol m-3 field
  integrates exactly with `convert_concentration_to_content`, with no density
  in the arithmetic. Handing it a µmol kg-1 field without an explicit density
  applies the tool's constant default instead, which undoes any in-situ density
  used to make that field and biases the inventory by about one per cent.
#### Common mistakes
- Multiplying mol m-3 by 1000 and labelling the result "µmol/kg".
- Applying a µmol kg-1 threshold (for example a hypoxia threshold defined in
  µmol kg-1) to a mol m-3 field without conversion: every cell then falls below
  it, and the whole ocean is marked as depleted.
- Reading chlorophyll in kg m-3 as mg m-3. The two differ by a factor of 1e6.

### [common-comparison-mask] One comparison sample: a shared mask for model–observation and multi-model metrics
(type: method; severity: must)
#### Key points
- Regridding model output to a spherical grid "generally involves a land–sea
  mask distinct from the model native mask". [OMIP-phys A3] After remapping,
  each source covers its own set of cells, and coastal cells differ most.
- The GLODAPv2 mapped climatology is a global 1° × 1° product on 33 standard
  depth surfaces, mapped from the GLODAPv2 bottle data. [Lauvset et al. 2016]
  It shares the horizontal geometry of the WOA 1° grid, but not WOA's data
  coverage or its land-sea mask.
#### Checked in the local files
- GLODAPv2.2016b surface `TCO2` has 41320 valid cells on its 1° grid, and
  WOA23 `landsea_01` calls 1459 of them land: 442 north of 60°N, 179 south of
  60°S, the rest coastal cells at all latitudes. `build_comparison_mask` on the
  GLODAP field keeps 39861 cells with `ocean_only=True` and all 41320 with
  `ocean_only=False` (checked 2026-10-20).
#### Implications for this project
- A model is comparable with an observation, or with another model, only where
  both have data, and several models can be ranked against each other on a
  cell-by-cell score (a bias, an RMSE or the pattern correlation of one field)
  only where all of them do. Left to itself each pair covers a different set of
  cells, so such a score from one pair describes a different piece of ocean
  from the next, and a ranking between them means nothing. Put the fields on
  one grid, build one mask over all of them with `build_comparison_mask`, and
  apply it with `.where(mask)` to every field before any metric is taken.
- Where the shared mask does not apply: a pattern that is itself the result of
  an analysis on each source's own domain, such as the regression pattern of a
  variability mode (see `eof-modes-and-pdo-index`). Each mode was computed on
  its own source's cells, so masking the patterns afterwards to the other
  sources' coverage does not make them comparable. Correlate each with the
  reference pattern over the cells the two share (`analyze_correlation` leaves
  out the cells either one lacks), and compare the models on those
  correlations. Nor does a mask built on one level carry to another: coverage
  shrinks with depth, so a statistic at several depths takes its cells from the
  fields at each depth, not from the surface mask.
- The numbers of one cell-by-cell comparison come from one sample. Report the
  means that `compute_difference_metrics` returns over the shared sample
  (`mean_data`, `mean_reference`) together with the bias, not means computed
  separately over different cells. A global or regional mean of each source
  over its own ocean, with its own weights, is a different quantity (see
  `omip-native-grid-vs-regridded`): when the difference of such means is asked
  for, take each mean on its own grid and say that the difference is not the
  bias on the shared mask.
- `ocean_only=True`, the default of `build_comparison_mask`, is right only when
  WOA23 is part of the comparison. The check is made on the grid, so a
  GLODAP-against-models comparison on the shared 1° geometry silently inherits
  WOA23's coastline and drops cells where GLODAP has data and WOA23 says land.
  Those are among the hardest cells for a model to match, so dropping them
  flatters every model. Pass `ocean_only=False` when the reference is not WOA23.
- Report the cell counts the mask records alongside the metrics. A mask much
  smaller than the individual fields means the products disagree about where
  the ocean is, not that the models are poor.
#### Common mistakes
- Scoring each model on its own pairwise mask in a cell-by-cell comparison of
  one field, and then ranking the models on those scores.
- Applying the WOA23 land-sea mask to a comparison against GLODAP.

### [anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices] Anomalies and climate indices: which climatology to remove, which baseline, which season convention
(type: method; severity: must)
#### Key points
- CPC changed the base period of its Oceanic Niño Index because, with a warming
  trend in the Niño-3.4 region since 1950, episodes defined against a fixed base
  period "are increasingly incorporating longer-term trends that do not reflect
  interannual ENSO variability". The ONI now uses centred 30-year base periods,
  each applied to successive 5-year spans of the record. [CPC ONI change]
  The same index therefore exists on several baselines; `configs/datasets.yaml`
  lists the local CPC products and the baseline of each.
- The WOA23 seasonal climatologies are calendar quarters: winter January–March,
  spring April–June, summer July–September, autumn October–December. [WOA23
  documentation]
#### Implications for this project
- Which climatology is removed decides what an anomaly means.
  - Against the field's **own** climatology (`compute_anomaly_vs_own_climatology`,
    and `compute_enso_nino_index` on model output) whatever bias the field
    carries cancels. This is what an index, a trend, a composite or a comparison
    between two products needs.
  - Against the **observed** climatology (`compute_anomaly_vs_woa23`) the
    field's bias against observations stays inside the anomaly. This answers
    "how far is this field from the observed ocean". An observed climatology
    forced on a model index puts the model's mean-state bias into the index
    (see `cmip6-tos-bulk-top-cell`).
- The baseline is part of the answer. An anomaly is defined only relative to
  the period its climatology came from, and two series built on different
  baselines differ by an offset (one constant per calendar month) even when
  their variability is identical. Compute an index on the baseline of any
  published series it is compared with (`base_period`), and state the
  climatology and baseline used; the tools record both in the result's attrs.
  The CPC products in the registry differ in baseline and are not
  interchangeable.
- Niño 3.4 is the standard region for ENSO monitoring. A monthly index is
  matched against the climatology of its own calendar month, which removes the
  seasonal cycle.
- Seasons: DJF/MAM/JJA/SON is the usual (meteorological) climate definition,
  while the WOA23 seasonal fields are the calendar quarters JFM/AMJ/JAS/OND.
  The two are offset by a month and must not be mixed in one comparison
  (`season_convention` of `temporal_aggregate`; `freq='season'` of
  `compute_anomaly_vs_woa23` uses the WOA23 quarters).
- Events are defined operationally by persistence, not by eye: a warm or cold
  phase of a climate index, a marine heatwave or a hypoxic spell counts only
  when the series stays beyond the threshold for a minimum number of
  consecutive steps. A single step beyond the threshold is noise;
  `min_duration` of `detect_threshold_events` is what separates an event from
  a wobble.
- Scan a time series for outliers on its anomalies, not on its raw values. A
  raw ocean series is dominated by its seasonal cycle, so every summer maximum
  sits in the tail of the distribution and the "outliers" returned are simply
  the warm season.
#### Common mistakes
- Reading the offset between a reconstructed index and a published one built on
  another baseline as an error of the reconstruction.
- Comparing seasonal means built on the DJF and the JFM conventions.

### [omip-weighted-means-and-integrals] How OMIP defines global and regional means and integrals of ocean fields
(type: method; severity: should)
#### Key points
- Spatial integrals "are computed using all grid points within the region or
  line. There is no sub-sampling." [OMIP-phys A2, C1]
- Global mean of a 3-D tracer T [OMIP-phys H12.1]:
  - Boussinesq model, **volume-weighted**: ⟨T⟩ = Σ T dA dz / Σ dA dz (Eq. H29)
  - non-Boussinesq model, **mass-weighted**: ⟨T⟩ = Σ ρ T dA dz / Σ ρ dA dz
    (Eq. H28)
  The same forms hold for salinity (Eqs. H35–H36).
- Global mean SST and SSS are **area-weighted over the top level**:
  tosga = Σ θ(k=1) dA / Σ dA (Eq. H34, likewise sosga, Eq. H37).
  [OMIP-phys H13, H18]
- Because the terms of these averages vary in time, OMIP computes the official
  global means online each time step (products of time-dependent terms).
  [OMIP-phys H12.1, H17]
- MPIOM (MPI-ESM1.2) is hydrostatic and Boussinesq. [MPI §4]
#### Implications for this project
- Offline, with monthly fields: a global mean of `thetao` is
  Σ thetao · volcello / Σ volcello over valid ocean cells. A surface mean is
  Σ tos · areacello / Σ areacello. Unweighted `mean()` over (y, x) on a
  curvilinear grid is wrong. On a regular grid, unweighted means over latitude
  are also wrong.
- The weight must be restricted to the **same cells** as the valid data
  (NaN-aware weights). Otherwise the denominator counts land or missing cells.
- Volume weighting and mass weighting differ only through density variations.
  In-situ density varies by no more than about 2 % around 1035 kg m-3 over
  most of the ocean (OMIP-phys G3). For a Boussinesq model the volume-weighted
  form is the protocol form. The papers read here confirm the Boussinesq formulation only
  for MPIOM. For the other two models, state the assumption.
- A mean over a region follows the same rule, with the region mask applied on
  the model's own grid.
- Use a weighted mean for any physical average. A plain mean is right only
  when every element represents the same amount of ocean, which is rarely
  true: cells shrink towards the poles, and layer thicknesses vary by more than
  an order of magnitude with depth. With the tools: `compute_basic_stat` with
  `op='area_weighted_mean'` for a horizontal mean, and
  `op='volume_weighted_mean'` for any mean whose dimensions include depth (the
  mean temperature or concentration of a depth range). Its plain `op='mean'`
  weights every element equally.
- The same holds for every statistic taken over a map, not only for means: an
  unweighted RMSE, bias or pattern correlation counts a polar cell as heavily
  as a tropical one many times its size. `compute_difference_metrics` weights
  by area by default; `analyze_correlation` needs `weighting='area'` for a
  pattern correlation between two maps.
- A share of the ocean beyond a threshold is a share of its area (or volume),
  not of its cells, and missing cells must stay out of the denominator.
  `compute_exceedance_fraction` does both and returns the amount behind the
  percentage (m2 or m3), which belongs next to the percentage when the question
  is how much ocean.
- The mean of a ratio is not the ratio of the means. Averaging a cell-by-cell
  ratio such as N:P weights every cell's composition by its volume alone; the
  ratio of the two volume-weighted means weights each cell by its content and
  is pulled towards the richest water. Both are defensible, so state which one
  is reported. `compute_ratio` gives the cell-by-cell ratio; the ratio of means
  is the weighted mean of each field, divided.
#### Common mistakes
- Averaging a depth profile level by level with equal weight. Level thickness
  varies from 10 m to 250 m or more.
- Dividing an area-weighted sum by the total area of the region including
  land.

### [missing-values-and-land-cells] CMIP6 missing value 1e20, land cells, and fill values under scaling
(type: pitfall; severity: should)
#### Key points
- The CMIP6 Omon table header sets `missing_value: 1e20` (integers: −999).
  [CMOR Omon header]
- "All fields are reported as 'missing' over grid cells that are entirely
  land." `areacello` "should be zero or missing over land", and `deptho` is
  missing over land. [OMIP-phys A7, G7; CMOR Ofx]
- CF: missing data is any value outside `valid_range` or equal to
  `_FillValue`/`missing_value`. For packed data, test for missing values
  **before** applying `scale_factor`/`add_offset`. Scaling the default float
  fill value can overflow to infinity. Missing data is not allowed in
  coordinate variables. [CF 2.5.1]
#### Implications for this project
- xarray decodes 1e20 to NaN by default. The local files trigger a harmless
  "multiple fill values" warning, and both declared fill values decode to NaN.
  Code that reads with `netCDF4` directly, or opens with
  `mask_and_scale=False`, sees 1e20 at land and sub-bottom points. One such
  value makes a sum or mean meaningless.
- NaN-aware reductions are required, and the weights must be masked to the
  same cells (see `omip-weighted-means-and-integrals`).
- A value near 1e20 (or 9.97e36, the netCDF float default) in any output is
  an unmasked fill value, not a result.
- Masks can differ between variables of the same model. In the local
  historical files at the top level, first month, the valid cells of `uo` and
  `thetao` differ in 3019 cells for CESM2 (89115 vs 86096 valid) and in 968
  for MPI-ESM1-2-LR (35823 vs 36791). Build each field's valid mask from the
  field itself.
- A hand-rolled weighted reduction loses the land mask. Written as
  `(data * weights).sum('time') / weights.sum()`, an all-land column returns
  0.0 rather than NaN, because xarray's sum skips missing values; that zero
  then spreads into neighbouring ocean cells the moment the field is regridded.
  `temporal_aggregate` masks each cell's weights to the steps it has data, so a
  land cell stays missing.
- A hand-written threshold comparison counts land as "not exceeding": NaN
  compares as False, so land enters the denominator of any fraction.
  `compute_exceedance_fraction` leaves missing cells out of the numerator and
  the denominator.

### [cmip6-grid-labels] CMIP6 grid labels gn / gr and what they imply for the three local models
(type: dataset; severity: should)
#### Key points
- Grid labels [CVs grid_label]:
  - `gn`: "data reported on a model's native grid"
  - `gr`: "regridded data reported on the data provider's preferred target grid"
  - `gr1`, `gr2`, …: other regridded grids. `gnz`: zonal mean on the native
    latitude grid. `gm`: global mean.
- OMIP: structured-grid models should archive on the native grid. The same
  variable on native and spherical grids keeps the same CMOR name, and the
  two "can never coexist in the same file". GFDL described its plan to coarsen
  ocean output to a 1° spherical grid to reduce archive size. [OMIP-phys A3.3–A3.4]
- Models with a moving vertical coordinate (z\*, isopycnal, hybrid) have
  **time-dependent cell thickness and volume**. [OMIP-phys B1.2]
#### Checked in the local files
`grid_label` and `grid` global attributes of `thetao`, read on 2026-09-24:

| model | grid_label | grid attribute | nominal_resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| CESM2 | gn | "native gx1v7 displaced pole grid (384x320 latxlon)" | 100 km |
| GFDL-ESM4 | **gr** | "ocean data regridded from tripolar - nominal 0.5 deg latitude/longitude to 180,360; interpolation method: conserve_order1" | 1x1 degree |
| MPI-ESM1-2-LR | gn | "gn" | 250 km |
#### Implications for this project
- GFDL-ESM4 values are already remapped, so "native-grid" numbers for GFDL
  are numbers on its 1° archive grid. The land–sea mask is the regridded one,
  and budgets are only approximately conservative. The native hybrid layers
  (75) were remapped to 35 z levels, which is why `volcello` is monthly for
  GFDL-ESM4.
- Resolution differs by a factor of about 2.5 between CESM2 (100 km) and MPI
  (250 km). Features narrower than the coarse grid (boundary currents,
  equatorial undercurrent, coastal upwelling) are not comparable in amplitude.
- Within one model, variables can sit at different staggered points on the
  same gn grid (see `velocity-components-are-grid-relative`).

### [omip-native-grid-vs-regridded] When to work on the native grid and when regridding is acceptable
(type: method; severity: should)
#### Key points
- Two aims conflict: spherical-grid output is easy to map and difference,
  while native-grid output preserves conservation for budgets. "regridding …
  generally involves a land–sea mask distinct from the model native mask. When
  changing the land–sea mask it is difficult to ensure conservation even when
  using 'conservative' regridding methods." [OMIP-phys A3]
- The loss of conservation "may be of minor consequence for many metric-based
  analysis purposes. However, it sacrifices the needs of budget analyses."
  Regridding "merely for visualization purposes and/or to produce difference
  maps" can ignore non-conservation. [OMIP-phys A3, A3.2]
- Structured-grid models archive on their native grid. The standard spherical
  targets are the 1° or ¼° grids of the World Ocean Atlas. [OMIP-phys A3.3]
- In the vertical, models on z, z\*, p or p\* need no regridding. Coarsening to
  the 33 WOA standard levels is allowed, but "care should be given to models
  that do not share the same 5500 m depth as the World Ocean Atlas".
  [OMIP-phys A4, C2]
- For transports across sections (overturning, passages, heat transport) on
  non-spherical grids, compute along a native-grid "zig-zag" path that
  approximates the section rather than regridding fluxes. [OMIP-phys C4]
#### Implications for this project
- A good pattern for model-versus-observation work: compute every **number**
  (global and basin means, inventories, integrals) on each source's native grid
  with its own weights. Regrid to the WOA 1° grid only for **maps, pattern
  correlation and RMSE on a common mask**. Say which numbers come from which
  grid. The exception is an analysis whose result depends on the grid and is
  compared between sources, such as an EOF mode with its explained variance
  and its regression pattern (see `eof-modes-and-pdo-index`): put every
  source, the observations included, on one common grid before the analysis,
  and take its numbers there.
- After regridding a field for a cell-by-cell comparison, build one shared
  valid-data mask for all sources before computing pattern statistics. Coastal
  cells otherwise differ between the sources (see `common-comparison-mask`,
  which also says when a pair's own shared cells are the sample instead, as
  for the patterns of a variability mode).
- Regrid only for the cell-by-cell comparison. An average, a trend or an
  integral belongs on the field's own grid with its own cell areas, because
  regridding replaces those with the target's.
- Method in `regrid_horizontal`: 'conservative' is the right choice going to a
  coarser grid and before any area-weighted step; 'bilinear' is the fallback
  when a model's corner mesh is unusable; 'nearest_s2d' is for masks and other
  categorical fields. A regridded RMSE or pattern correlation depends on the
  method, so state the method whenever one is quoted.
- Vertical interpolation onto another level set changes layer-integrated
  quantities. Integrate thickness-weighted quantities (inventories, OMZ
  thickness) on the original levels.
- Values at fixed depths (a bias "at 100, 500 and 1000 m") need each product
  interpolated to those depths (`regrid_vertical`), not the nearest level
  selected: vertical grids differ between products and rarely line up, so each
  product would answer at a different depth, and the products differ most
  where the gradients are steepest. This interpolation is for comparison, not
  for budgets.
- Diagnostics located at a vertical extremum, such as a nutricline (the depth of
  the maximum vertical gradient), are quantised by the vertical grid. Where the
  profile is smooth, in the subtropical gyres especially, the maximum can jump
  between levels, so read such a map together with the gradient itself.
#### Common mistakes
- Computing a global inventory after regridding to 1° and comparing it with
  the native-grid value as if they should agree.
- Regridding velocity components as scalars (see
  `velocity-components-are-grid-relative`).

===== USER =====
User question: Evaluate the ocean carbon system of three CMIP6 models against the GLODAPv2.2016b mapped climatology: dissolved inorganic carbon, total alkalinity and pH.

Data to use:
- CMIP6 historical `dissic`, `talk`, `ph`, `thetao` and `so` from CESM2, GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR, monthly, for the calendar year 2002, plus `dissic`, `thetao` and `so` for 1971-2000.
- Each model's own areacello and volcello.
- GLODAPv2.2016b mapped climatology: TCO2, TAlk and pH.
