### [cmip6-bgc-units-per-volume] CMIP6 biogeochemical tracers are per unit volume (mol m-3, kg m-3)
(type: concept; severity: must)
#### Key points
- In the OMIP tracer equation "conservation of volume is assumed … and
  standard units of mol m−3 are used for all tracers". [OMIP-BGC §2.4]
- The CMOR comments define "mole concentration" as "number of moles per unit
  volume, also called 'molarity'". [CMOR Omon, o2/no3/po4/si]
- Units as archived [CMOR Omon; OMIP-BGC Tables 6, 10, 12]:

  | variable | CMOR units | common reporting unit | factor (volume basis) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | o2, o2sat, no3, po4, si, dissic, dissoc, talk, phycos | mol m-3 | mmol m-3 = µmol L-1 | × 1e3 |
  | dfe | mol m-3 | nmol L-1 (nM) | × 1e6 |
  | chl | kg m-3 | mg m-3 (≈ µg L-1) | × 1e6 |
  | ph | 1 | – | – |

  The factors in the last column are unit arithmetic. They change the prefix
  but keep the volume basis.
#### Implications for this project
- Numbers in mmol m-3 are **still per volume**. WOA23 and GLODAP report
  µmol kg-1 (per mass). Changing the prefix does not make the two comparable.
  Only a density conversion does: µmol kg-1 = mmol m-3 × 1000 / ρ, where ρ is in
  kg m-3. `configs/datasets.yaml` (`cross_dataset_notes.units`) records this
  and notes that no loader converts automatically.
- The density error only cancels in quantities that are invariant under scaling:
  ratios of two tracers converted with the same ρ, or depths of extrema and
  gradients. Absolute concentrations, inventories against observations and
  thresholds on a mass basis all need the conversion. The scale-invariant
  quantities need no conversion at all and are best taken from the native
  field: a constant ρ cancels exactly, while the in-situ density, which grows
  with depth, rescales each level by a different factor and so itself shifts
  a depth located from the shape of a profile (a nutricline, the depth of a
  maximum gradient) by a little.
- Inventories (mol) are volume integrals of the per-volume field: Σ c · volcello.
  No density is needed for these on the model side.
- Two concentrations on different bases cannot be compared, differenced or
  evaluated against each other until one has been converted. The conversion
  needs a density, and the one that mass-per-volume arithmetic needs is the
  in-situ density, `compute_potential_density_anomaly` with `ref_pressure=None`
  (a sigma at a fixed reference pressure is not). `transform_unit` defaults to a
  constant density, the mean surface value, which errs by about a per cent in
  the deep ocean and in fresh polar water, enough to rival the differences a
  model-observation comparison sets out to measure. Pass the in-situ density
  field for anything below the surface that needs converting (not for the
  scale-invariant quantities above), and state which density was used (the
  result's `attrs['density_assumption']`).
- For inventories prefer the volume basis: a model's native mol m-3 field
  integrates exactly with `convert_concentration_to_content`, with no density
  in the arithmetic. Handing it a µmol kg-1 field without an explicit density
  applies the tool's constant default instead, which undoes any in-situ density
  used to make that field and biases the inventory by about one per cent.
#### Common mistakes
- Multiplying mol m-3 by 1000 and labelling the result "µmol/kg".
- Applying a µmol kg-1 threshold (for example a hypoxia threshold defined in
  µmol kg-1) to a mol m-3 field without conversion: every cell then falls below
  it, and the whole ocean is marked as depleted.
- Reading chlorophyll in kg m-3 as mg m-3. The two differ by a factor of 1e6.

### [common-comparison-mask] One comparison sample: a shared mask for model–observation and multi-model metrics
(type: method; severity: must)
#### Key points
- Regridding model output to a spherical grid "generally involves a land–sea
  mask distinct from the model native mask". [OMIP-phys A3] After remapping,
  each source covers its own set of cells, and coastal cells differ most.
- The GLODAPv2 mapped climatology is a global 1° × 1° product on 33 standard
  depth surfaces, mapped from the GLODAPv2 bottle data. [Lauvset et al. 2016]
  It shares the horizontal geometry of the WOA 1° grid, but not WOA's data
  coverage or its land-sea mask.
#### Checked in the local files
- GLODAPv2.2016b surface `TCO2` has 41320 valid cells on its 1° grid, and
  WOA23 `landsea_01` calls 1459 of them land: 442 north of 60°N, 179 south of
  60°S, the rest coastal cells at all latitudes. `build_comparison_mask` on the
  GLODAP field keeps 39861 cells with `ocean_only=True` and all 41320 with
  `ocean_only=False` (checked 2026-10-20).
#### Implications for this project
- A model is comparable with an observation, or with another model, only where
  both have data, and several models can be ranked against each other on a
  cell-by-cell score (a bias, an RMSE or the pattern correlation of one field)
  only where all of them do. Left to itself each pair covers a different set of
  cells, so such a score from one pair describes a different piece of ocean
  from the next, and a ranking between them means nothing. Put the fields on
  one grid, build one mask over all of them with `build_comparison_mask`, and
  apply it with `.where(mask)` to every field before any metric is taken.
- Where the shared mask does not apply: a pattern that is itself the result of
  an analysis on each source's own domain, such as the regression pattern of a
  variability mode (see `eof-modes-and-pdo-index`). Each mode was computed on
  its own source's cells, so masking the patterns afterwards to the other
  sources' coverage does not make them comparable. Correlate each with the
  reference pattern over the cells the two share (`analyze_correlation` leaves
  out the cells either one lacks), and compare the models on those
  correlations. Nor does a mask built on one level carry to another: coverage
  shrinks with depth, so a statistic at several depths takes its cells from the
  fields at each depth, not from the surface mask.
- The numbers of one cell-by-cell comparison come from one sample. Report the
  means that `compute_difference_metrics` returns over the shared sample
  (`mean_data`, `mean_reference`) together with the bias, not means computed
  separately over different cells. A global or regional mean of each source
  over its own ocean, with its own weights, is a different quantity (see
  `omip-native-grid-vs-regridded`): when the difference of such means is asked
  for, take each mean on its own grid and say that the difference is not the
  bias on the shared mask.
- `ocean_only=True`, the default of `build_comparison_mask`, is right only when
  WOA23 is part of the comparison. The check is made on the grid, so a
  GLODAP-against-models comparison on the shared 1° geometry silently inherits
  WOA23's coastline and drops cells where GLODAP has data and WOA23 says land.
  Those are among the hardest cells for a model to match, so dropping them
  flatters every model. Pass `ocean_only=False` when the reference is not WOA23.
- Report the cell counts the mask records alongside the metrics. A mask much
  smaller than the individual fields means the products disagree about where
  the ocean is, not that the models are poor.
#### Common mistakes
- Scoring each model on its own pairwise mask in a cell-by-cell comparison of
  one field, and then ranking the models on those scores.
- Applying the WOA23 land-sea mask to a comparison against GLODAP.

### [anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices] Anomalies and climate indices: which climatology to remove, which baseline, which season convention
(type: method; severity: must)
#### Key points
- CPC changed the base period of its Oceanic Niño Index because, with a warming
  trend in the Niño-3.4 region since 1950, episodes defined against a fixed base
  period "are increasingly incorporating longer-term trends that do not reflect
  interannual ENSO variability". The ONI now uses centred 30-year base periods,
  each applied to successive 5-year spans of the record. [CPC ONI change]
  The same index therefore exists on several baselines; `configs/datasets.yaml`
  lists the local CPC products and the baseline of each.
- The WOA23 seasonal climatologies are calendar quarters: winter January–March,
  spring April–June, summer July–September, autumn October–December. [WOA23
  documentation]
#### Implications for this project
- Which climatology is removed decides what an anomaly means.
  - Against the field's **own** climatology (`compute_anomaly_vs_own_climatology`,
    and `compute_enso_nino_index` on model output) whatever bias the field
    carries cancels. This is what an index, a trend, a composite or a comparison
    between two products needs.
  - Against the **observed** climatology (`compute_anomaly_vs_woa23`) the
    field's bias against observations stays inside the anomaly. This answers
    "how far is this field from the observed ocean". An observed climatology
    forced on a model index puts the model's mean-state bias into the index
    (see `cmip6-tos-bulk-top-cell`).
- The baseline is part of the answer. An anomaly is defined only relative to
  the period its climatology came from, and two series built on different
  baselines differ by an offset (one constant per calendar month) even when
  their variability is identical. Compute an index on the baseline of any
  published series it is compared with (`base_period`), and state the
  climatology and baseline used; the tools record both in the result's attrs.
  The CPC products in the registry differ in baseline and are not
  interchangeable.
- Niño 3.4 is the standard region for ENSO monitoring. A monthly index is
  matched against the climatology of its own calendar month, which removes the
  seasonal cycle.
- Seasons: DJF/MAM/JJA/SON is the usual (meteorological) climate definition,
  while the WOA23 seasonal fields are the calendar quarters JFM/AMJ/JAS/OND.
  The two are offset by a month and must not be mixed in one comparison
  (`season_convention` of `temporal_aggregate`; `freq='season'` of
  `compute_anomaly_vs_woa23` uses the WOA23 quarters).
- Events are defined operationally by persistence, not by eye: a warm or cold
  phase of a climate index, a marine heatwave or a hypoxic spell counts only
  when the series stays beyond the threshold for a minimum number of
  consecutive steps. A single step beyond the threshold is noise;
  `min_duration` of `detect_threshold_events` is what separates an event from
  a wobble.
- Scan a time series for outliers on its anomalies, not on its raw values. A
  raw ocean series is dominated by its seasonal cycle, so every summer maximum
  sits in the tail of the distribution and the "outliers" returned are simply
  the warm season.
#### Common mistakes
- Reading the offset between a reconstructed index and a published one built on
  another baseline as an error of the reconstruction.
- Comparing seasonal means built on the DJF and the JFM conventions.

### [monthly-means-are-time-averages] CMIP6 monthly fields are time averages of every model time step, so nonlinear diagnostics from them are not the model's own
(type: pitfall; severity: must)
#### Key points
- Each requested period (day, month, year) "is sampled as a time average. Time
  averages include all model time steps over the given period; there is no
  sub-sampling. Products of time-dependent fields are time-averaged as a
  product". [OMIP-phys A1]
- `mlotstmax`/`mlotstmin` are the extremes over snapshots within the month.
  Adjacent points "need not have extrema registered at the same time step".
  [OMIP-phys A1, H24.1]
- Squared fields (e.g. `tossq`, `mlotstsq`) are accumulated online so that
  variance can be computed. Squaring a monthly mean does not give them.
  [OMIP-phys Table H1 caption, H25]
- MLD is recommended online "in order to avoid aliasing". [OMIP-phys H24.2]
- For extensive quantities, "experience at GFDL with Boussinesq z* models
  suggests that errors are minor when working offline using time averaged
  thickness and time averaged tracer concentration". Errors are "generally
  unacceptable" for isopycnal models. [OMIP-phys F3]
#### Implications for this project
- f(monthly mean) ≠ monthly mean of f for any nonlinear f. Examples are density
  from T and S, MLD by a threshold crossing, depth of a maximum gradient, a
  threshold exceedance (hypoxia) or a ratio of two tracers. The order of
  operations therefore changes the answer and must be chosen on purpose:
  - **diagnostic first, then average** reproduces the mean of the diagnostic
    and matches how archived online diagnostics are defined;
  - **average first, then diagnostic** gives the diagnostic of the mean state.
    This is the like-for-like choice when the reference exists only as a
    climatology.
  State which order was used and why.
- Monthly variance within a month cannot be recovered from monthly means. Only
  interannual or inter-month variance can.
- Averaging monthly fields over a period needs day weights in the model's own
  calendar. `temporal_aggregate` does this, and equal weights do not: calendar
  months are 28 to 31 days long, so an equal-weight average of monthly means is
  not a time average. `compute_basic_stat(op='mean', dim='time')` gives every
  month the same weight. `temporal_aggregate` is the only tool that weights by
  month length and so the only correct way to average monthly data over a
  period that spans different calendar months; its `freq='mean'` gives the
  mean state of a period, the field a bias or an RMSE is measured on. Day
  weights do not enter a monthly climatology, each calendar month averaged
  across the years of a baseline (what an anomaly or a climate index is taken
  against): that is the plain mean of that month's values over the years, as
  climate normals are defined, and `compute_anomaly_vs_own_climatology` and the
  index tools build it so.
#### Common mistakes
- Calling an MLD from climatological T/S profiles "the model's climatological
  MLD". It is a different quantity from the time mean of the monthly MLDs.
- Reporting a variance of monthly means as if it were sub-monthly variability.

### [historical-ssp245-member-continuity] Joining historical and ssp245 only makes one continuous run when the member matches the scenario's parent
(type: pitfall; severity: must)
#### Key points
- `ssp245` has `parent_experiment_id: historical` and runs 2015–2100.
  `historical` runs 1850–2014 with parent `piControl` (or past1000/past2k).
  [CVs experiment_id]
- The 11 CESM2(CAM6) historical members start from **different years of the
  piControl run** (years 501, 601, 631, …, 871). They are independent
  realisations of internal variability, not copies. [CESM2 §4]
#### Checked in the local files
These are the global attributes of the first `thetao`/`o2` file in each
directory, read on 2026-09-24:

| model | historical variant (parent) | ssp245 variant → parent_variant_label |
|---|---|---|
| CESM2 | r1i1p1f1 (piControl r1i1p1f1) | **r10i1p1f1 → historical r10i1p1f1** |
| GFDL-ESM4 | r1i1p1f1 | r1i1p1f1 → historical r1i1p1f1 |
| MPI-ESM1-2-LR | r1i1p1f1 | r1i1p1f1 → historical r1i1p1f1 |

The local archive does **not** contain CESM2 historical r10i1p1f1.
#### Implications for this project
- For GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR, historical r1 followed by ssp245 r1 is one
  continuous trajectory. A time series across 2014/2015 is valid.
- For CESM2, historical (r1) and ssp245 (r10) are **different realisations**.
  - A future-minus-historical difference (a late-21st-century window minus a
    late-20th-century window)
    contains forced change **plus** the difference in internal variability
    between two members. For long averaging windows and large forced signals
    the extra term is small, but it is not zero. State it.
  - A trend fitted across the join, or a plot that joins the two, shows a
    spurious discontinuity at 2015. Do not fit across the join for CESM2.
- The same mismatch occurs within one experiment: CESM2 historical `phycos` is
  r6 and MPI historical `ph` is r10 (see `configs/datasets.yaml`). Joint
  point-by-point analysis with other variables mixes members.
#### Common mistakes
- Concatenating CESM2 historical and ssp245 and fitting one trend over
  1850–2100.
- Reporting a CESM2 scenario change without noting that the baseline comes
  from another member.

### [cmip6-o2sat-and-derived-oxygen] CMIP6 o2 and o2sat, and deriving AOU and percent saturation from them
(type: concept; severity: should)
#### Key points
- `o2` is "Dissolved Oxygen Concentration" and `o2sat` is "Dissolved Oxygen
  Concentration at Saturation". Both are mol m-3 on (lon, lat, olevel, time).
  `o2sat` means "the mole concentration in a saturated solution". [CMOR Omon]
- For oxygen, OMIP takes the saturation concentration [O2]⁰_sat from **Garcia
  and Gordon (1992)** (Eq. 8, Table 1). The atmospheric pressure correction is
  [A]_sat = (P_a / P⁰_a) · [A]⁰_sat, with atmospheric O2 fixed at a mole
  fraction of 0.20946. [OMIP-BGC §2.1.2, §2.5.3]
- Surface pressure variations "must not be neglected". Between 60°S and 30°S the
  mean surface pressure is about 3 % below the global mean, which lowers
  [O2]_sat there by about 10 µmol kg-1. [OMIP-BGC §2.5.3]
- The data request also defines `o2min` (oxygen minimum concentration) and
  `zo2min` (its depth, the shallower one if there are two). [CMOR Omon;
  OMIP-BGC Table 15]
#### Implications for this project
- Per model, AOU = `o2sat` − `o2` and percent saturation = 100 · `o2` /
  `o2sat` are consistent derivations (same grid, same member, same per-volume
  basis). The volume basis cancels in the percent saturation but **not** in
  AOU.
- Against WOA23 AOU or percent saturation, the model and observed saturation
  values may rest on different solubility conventions and different pressure
  assumptions. The model value follows the OMIP recipe above, while the WOA
  convention has to be taken from WOA documentation. Treat the differences as
  including a definitional component. For AOU, convert to µmol kg-1 first.
- Low-oxygen thresholds are usually stated in µmol kg-1, commonly 60 for
  hypoxia and 5 for suboxia. `identify_oxygen_minimum_zone` compares the field
  with the threshold in the field's own units, so model `o2` (mol m-3) has to
  be converted first (see `cmip6-bgc-units-per-volume`).
- Availability is uneven: CESM2 has no `o2`/`o2sat` in historical, only in
  ssp245 (`configs/datasets.yaml`). `o2min`/`zo2min` are not in the local
  archive and must be derived from `o2` (see `derive-unavailable-diagnostics`).
#### Common mistakes
- Computing AOU from a model `o2` field and an observational saturation field
  (or the reverse). The two fields are on different bases and conventions.
- Reporting model AOU in mol m-3 next to WOA AOU in µmol kg-1.

### [cmip6-static-grid-fields] Meaning of the CMIP6 ocean grid fields areacello, volcello, deptho, sftof (and the absent thkcello/masscello)
(type: concept; severity: should)
#### Key points
- **areacello**: "Horizontal area of ocean grid cells" (m2, cell_methods
  `area: sum`). This is the area of a **tracer** cell, and it "should be zero or
  missing over land". All CMIP6 ocean models have fixed horizontal grids, so
  the area is static. [CMOR Ofx; OMIP-phys B, G7]
- **volcello**: "Ocean Grid-Cell Volume" (m3, `area: sum`). This is an
  extensive field. It exists as a fixed field (Ofx) and as a time-varying one
  (Omon). [CMOR Ofx, Omon]
- **deptho**: "sea floor depth for present day relative to z=0 geoid.
  Reported as missing for land grid cells." It is the bottom "from the
  perspective of the model tracer fields". [CMOR Ofx; OMIP-phys G5]
- **sftof**: "Percentage of horizontal area occupied by ocean" (%). [CMOR Ofx]
- **thkcello** (cell thickness, m) and **masscello** (mass per area, kg m-2)
  are part of the request. For models with static cells,
  masscello = rhozero × thkcello, where rhozero is the model's Boussinesq
  reference density. That density is not the same in all models: OMIP
  suggests 1035 kg m-3, and early GFDL models used 1000 kg m-3. Where cell
  thickness varies in time, both are monthly fields. [CMOR Ofx/Omon; OMIP-phys
  B1–B2, G3]
- For full cells, thickness can be taken as the difference of the layer
  depth bounds. That fails at partial top and bottom cells. [OMIP-phys B1.1]
#### Implications for this project
- None of the three models' local archive has `thkcello`, `masscello` or `rhozero`.
  The layer thickness that respects partial bottom cells is
  **volcello / areacello**. The `lev_bnds` difference is right only for full
  cells. Checked on the local historical files (January 2000, cells where
  `thetao` is valid):
  - **GFDL-ESM4**: area × layer-bound thickness gives 1.53e18 m3 against
    1.33e18 m3 from `volcello`, and 14 % of cells differ by more than 1 %.
    Its remapped z levels carry values in bottom cells that are only partly
    water.
  - **MPI-ESM1-2-LR**: partial bottom steps differ by up to ±50 % in 3.3 % of
    cells.
  - **CESM2**: the two agree within 0.6 %.
  `cell_thickness_like` in `src/tools/data_loader.py` returns volcello /
  areacello for CMIP6 fields. `identify_oxygen_minimum_zone` and the depth
  averages use it.
- `deptho` is model bathymetry, not true bathymetry. Deciding "is this level
  above the model sea floor" should use `deptho` or the fill pattern of the
  field itself, not an external topography.
- GFDL-ESM4's `volcello` varies in time because its native vertical coordinate
  moves (see `cmip6-grid-labels` and `configs/datasets.yaml`). The loaders
  already handle the time axis.
- On a curvilinear grid the model's own `areacello` is the only correct area
  weight, and the published `volcello` alone accounts for the partial cells at
  the sea floor. `load_areas` and `load_volumes_for_cmip6` use them where the
  model publishes them and record the origin in `attrs['area_source']` and
  `attrs['volume_source']`, which is worth reporting when they feed an
  integral.
- Total ocean area differs between models by a per cent or so, because a
  coarser grid resolves the coastline differently. That is a property of the
  model, not an error to correct: state the area each integral was taken over
  rather than rescaling one model to match another.

### [basin-and-region-definitions] Ocean basin partitions in CMIP6/OMIP and CF standard region names
(type: method; severity: should)
#### Key points
- OMIP recommends a tracer region mask with CF standard region names and
  `flag_values = 1..10` [OMIP-phys G6]:

  | flag | region | flag | region |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | southern_ocean | 6 | mediterranean_sea |
  | 2 | atlantic_ocean | 7 | black_sea |
  | 3 | pacific_ocean | 8 | hudson_bay |
  | 4 | arctic_ocean | 9 | baltic_sea |
  | 5 | indian_ocean | 10 | red_sea |

  Some enclosed seas "may be missing from the model". [OMIP-phys G6]
- The CMOR Ofx `basin` variable (standard_name `region`) carries this mask on
  the model grid. It is requested on both the native and the spherical grid.
  [CMOR Ofx; OMIP-phys Table G1]
- For **overturning and meridional transports** the partition is different:
  `atlantic_arctic_ocean`, `indian_pacific_ocean`, `global_ocean`.
  [CMOR coordinate `basin`; OMIP-phys I6]
- The CMOR `oline` axis lists the standard passages (e.g. drake_passage,
  indonesian_throughflow, bering_strait, fram_strait, denmark_strait,
  florida_bahamas_strait, pacific_equatorial_undercurrent). [CMOR coordinate]
- CF: regions with complex boundaries are identified by a label variable with
  standard_name `region`, using the standardised region names. [CF 6.1.1]
#### Implications for this project
- The Ofx `basin` field is not in the local archive. Basin statistics here use
  the WOA23 basin mask carried onto each grid (`select_basin`,
  `load_basin_mask`). That mask follows a WOA labelling, not the OMIP 10-region
  one. Report which mask was used, whether the Southern Ocean is a separate
  basin or split among the three oceans, and whether marginal seas are
  folded into their parent ocean. Each of these choices changes basin means.
- In the WOA23 mask each marginal sea carries its own code, so an ocean taken
  as its main code alone silently excludes a large part of what the name
  usually means. `select_basin` folds the marginal seas into the ocean they
  open onto by default (`include_marginal_seas=True`); either choice is
  defensible as long as it is stated.
- A marginal sea's label stops at its sill, and below that depth the water
  belongs to the open ocean, so the mask labels each standard level
  separately. The surface labels applied through the whole water column
  (`depth_dependent=False`) are what a named ocean usually means; each level's
  own labels (`depth_dependent=True`) are what a water-mass analysis wants.
- Model domains differ: the CESM2 paper notes that the Caspian Sea is treated
  as a lake in the land model and "is no longer included in the ocean model"
  (see `model-cesm2-configuration`). Marginal seas present in an observational
  mask may therefore have no model counterpart.
#### Common mistakes
- Reporting "Indian Ocean" overturning. OMIP says the Indian and Pacific
  basins cannot be separated for overturning.
- Comparing basin means computed with different basin masks for model and
  observations.

===== USER =====
User question: Assess the global oxygen minimum zones (OMZ) simulated by CMIP6 models against the WOA23 observed climatology, and quantify how they change under the ssp245 scenario.

Data to use:
- CMIP6 historical `o2`, `thetao` and `so` from GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR, monthly, 1971-01 to 2000-12. CESM2 publishes no `o2` for the historical experiment.

<knowledge_notes>
[cmip6-bgc-units-per-volume]
- Model `o2` is mol m-3: convert to µmol kg-1 as µmol kg-1 = mmol m-3 × 1000/ρ using the in-situ density (`compute_potential_density_anomaly`, `ref_pressure=None`), not `transform_unit`'s constant default; state the density used (`attrs['density_assumption']`).
- The total O2 inventory in moles is a volume integral of the native mol m-3 field with `convert_concentration_to_content` and `volcello` — no density; passing a converted µmol kg-1 field instead applies the default density and biases the inventory by about 1%.

[common-comparison-mask]
- Comparing global OMZ volumes is a difference of means each taken over its own domain, not a bias on a shared mask; report each on its own grid and say so. `ocean_only=True` is right because WOA23 is the reference.
- Build any mask per depth level (coverage shrinks with depth) and report the cell counts alongside the metrics.

[anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices] not applicable: no anomaly removal, climate index, baseline choice or seasonal aggregation is requested; the scenario change is a difference of two 30-year period means.

[monthly-means-are-time-averages]
- A threshold exceedance is nonlinear: state the order used — diagnostic-then-average (per-month OMZ volume, then period mean) or average-then-diagnostic (OMZ of the period-mean field, the like-for-like choice against the WOA23 climatology).
- Average monthly fields over the periods with `temporal_aggregate(freq='mean')` for day weights; `compute_basic_stat(op='mean', dim='time')` weights every calendar month equally and is not a time average.

[historical-ssp245-member-continuity]
- GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR historical r1i1p1f1 followed by ssp245 r1i1p1f1 is one continuous trajectory, so their period difference is valid; CESM2 ssp245 is r10i1p1f1 and historical r10i1p1f1 is absent, so no change can be formed for CESM2 — label it as such.

[cmip6-o2sat-and-derived-oxygen]
- Convert model `o2` to µmol kg-1 before thresholding: `identify_oxygen_minimum_zone` compares the field with the threshold in the field's own units (the 20 and 60 µmol kg-1 levels).
- WOA23 AOU and percent saturation rest on WOA's own solubility and pressure conventions; state that differences from the model's OMIP recipe are partly definitional.

[cmip6-static-grid-fields]
- OMZ volume = Σ `volcello` over cells below the threshold; column thickness uses `cell_thickness_like` (volcello/areacello), which respects partial bottom cells — `lev_bnds` differences fail there (GFDL-ESM4: 14% of cells differ by >1%; MPI partial steps up to ±50% in 3.3% of cells).
- `deptho` is model bathymetry; total ocean area differs by about a per cent between models, so report the domain each integral covers rather than rescaling.

[basin-and-region-definitions]
- The Ofx `basin` field is not in the local archive: take basins from the WOA23 mask via `select_basin`/`load_basin_mask` with `include_marginal_seas=True` (folds marginal seas into the parent ocean) and state the mask used.
- State the `depth_dependent` choice: surface labels applied through the column (`False`) is what a named ocean usually means; per-level labels (`True`) are what a water-mass analysis wants.
