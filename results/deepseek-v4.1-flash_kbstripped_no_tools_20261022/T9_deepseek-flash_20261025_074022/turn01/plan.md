# Plan

1. Load `cmip6_historical` Omon `tos` for CESM2, GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR, monthly 1950-01 to 2014-12, plus each model’s Ofx `areacello`; load `ersst` monthly `sst` for the same months; load `cpc_indices` product `pdo` for the same months. Inspect units, longitude conventions and time axes, and keep model-specific missing-variable handling as supplied by the dataset.
- Note [cmip6-tos-bulk-top-cell]: `tos` is the bulk top-cell temperature including under sea ice, where near-freezing values are valid data and not missing; use the top cell, not a 10 m level, and never give a model index an observed climatology.

2. Put all SST fields on the common 1-degree grid before the PDO analysis: bilinearly regrid each model’s `tos` and the ERSSTv5 `sst` to the same 1-degree latitude-longitude grid, preserving monthly time and masking land/ocean consistently on that grid.
- Note [omip-native-grid-vs-regridded]: regrid all four sources, observations included, to the one 1-degree grid before computing EOFs, explained variance or regression patterns, and state that the horizontal method is bilinear; EOF metrics are grid-dependent.

3. For each source separately, compute the 1950-2014 monthly climatology on the 1-degree grid as the plain mean of each calendar month across all years, then subtract that calendar-month climatology from the corresponding monthly SST to form monthly anomalies.
- Note [monthly-means-are-time-averages]: the 1950-2014 monthly climatology is the plain mean of each calendar month across years; day weights do not enter it.
- Note [anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices]: use each source’s own 1950-2014 monthly climatology, models and ERSSTv5 alike, so mean-state bias cancels and an offset between baselines is not an error; match each monthly index to its own calendar month and do not mix seasonal conventions.

4. Remove the global-mean SST anomaly from each source’s anomaly field: compute each source’s area-weighted global-mean SST anomaly time series over 1950-2014, using each CMIP6 model’s own `areacello` fixed field for its global mean and cos-latitude cell areas for ERSSTv5, then subtract that time series from every grid cell.

5. For each source, restrict to the PDO domain 20N-70N, 110E-100W, represented on the 1-degree grid as 110E-260E, and form the monthly anomaly matrix. Compute the leading EOF with area weights `sqrt(cos(latitude))`, take the leading principal component, fix its sign so positive index means negative SST anomalies in the central and western North Pacific, and standardize the index to zero mean and unit variance. Compute the regression pattern by regressing grid-cell anomalies on the standardized index, giving degrees Celsius per standard deviation; define the mode amplitude as the area-weighted RMS of that regression pattern over the PDO domain using cos-latitude area weights. Record the leading-mode explained-variance percentage, the regression pattern and the amplitude.
- Note [eof-modes-and-pdo-index]: anomalies are SST minus the climatological annual cycle and minus the global-mean SST anomaly at each grid point; the EOF uses area weights `sqrt(cos(latitude))`, the sign is fixed by the central and western North Pacific SST-anomaly convention, and the index is standardized; the EOF itself is dimensionless, so report physical amplitude from the regression pattern and its area-weighted RMS, and compare explained variance only between fields on the same grid over the same period.

6. For each standardized monthly index, compute the lag-1-year autocorrelation at 12 months over 1950-2014 and report it to 3 decimal places.
- Note [serial-correlation-and-significance]: report the 12-month lag autocorrelation as the persistence figure as is; for the ERSSTv5-derived index versus the published PDO use the series as they are with no detrending and quote the effective-sample-size-corrected significance; for spatial pattern correlations read the coefficient rather than its p-value because no spatial correction exists.

7. Align the ERSSTv5-derived standardized index with the published `pdo` monthly series over 1950-01 to 2014-12 and compute their Pearson correlation to 3 decimal places, with the effective-sample-size-corrected significance, following the [serial-correlation-and-significance] treatment. Interpret the correlation as agreement in temporal phase and variability of the ERSST-derived leading mode with the published PDO over this window; it does not by itself prove that the EOF is uniquely the PDO, does not validate the CMIP6 models, and does not establish a causal mechanism, and the shared trend remains part of the agreement because no detrending is applied.

8. For each model, compare its regression pattern with the ERSSTv5 regression pattern over the PDO-domain cells shared by the two products, using an area-weighted Pearson pattern correlation with cos-latitude weights; report the shared-cell count alongside each correlation and rank the models on these correlations. Do not mask a model pattern to ERSSTv5 coverage after its EOF; compute the correlation only over the cells both patterns have.
- Note [common-comparison-mask]: correlate each model’s pattern with the ERSSTv5 pattern over cells the two share using area weighting, report shared-cell counts, and interpret a shared mask much smaller than the individual fields as a coverage or ocean-mask disagreement rather than a model-skill result.

9. Using only the ERSSTv5 standardized index, compute the 9-year (108-month) centred running mean, identify calendar years in which that smoothed curve changes sign, and merge any crossing less than 3 years after the last counted transition into that earlier transition year. Report the resulting observed PDO phase-transition years over 1950-2014 and match them against documented Pacific climate shifts.
- Note [cmip6-experiments-historical-ssp245]: historical CMIP6 runs carry their own single-member internal variability, so individual years and event timing are not comparable; compare model statistics such as explained variance, amplitude and persistence, and define transition years only for the ERSSTv5 index.

10. Assess the models by combining the pattern-correlation ranking from step 8 with comparisons of each model’s amplitude, explained variance and lag-12 autocorrelation against the ERSSTv5 values; state which model reproduces the observed pattern best and whether the models’ amplitude and persistence are consistent with observations, without treating individual historical years as event matches.

Other notes:
- The published PDO product is a dimensionless pattern index; its correlation with the ERSSTv5-derived index demonstrates temporal agreement between the two monthly series over 1950-2014, but not that the computed EOF is the unique PDO, not CMIP6 model skill, and not causality.
- All computed anomalies use each source’s own 1950-2014 monthly climatology; an offset between series from different baselines is not an error.
- Explained variance, amplitude and regression patterns are reported on the common 1-degree bilinear grid over 1950-2014, because EOF metrics depend on grid and period.
- Pattern correlations are spatial shared-cell correlations; their p-values are not used, and the shared-cell counts should be read with the correlation values.
- CMIP6 historical runs are single-member, so model evaluation uses statistics only, not individual-year event timing; transition years are defined only from the ERSSTv5 index.
