**Direct answer.** Using the executed workflow, the ERSSTv5-derived leading North Pacific SST mode explains **24.7%** of variance, has an amplitude of **0.335 °C per standard deviation**, and a 12-month lag autocorrelation of **0.351**. Its standardized monthly index correlates **0.958** with the published PDO series. Among the three CMIP6 models, **CESM2 reproduces the observed PDO regression pattern best** (area-weighted shared-cell pattern correlation **0.928**), followed by GFDL-ESM4 (**0.893**) and MPI-ESM1-2-LR (**0.763**). CESM2 also has the closest amplitude and explained variance, but its persistence is substantially weaker than observed; GFDL-ESM4 has the closest persistence but lower amplitude and much lower explained variance; MPI-ESM1-2-LR has stronger amplitude and persistence but the weakest spatial pattern agreement.

**Methodology actually used.** For each source, monthly SST anomalies were formed on the native grid using that source’s own 1950–2014 calendar-month climatology, computed as the plain mean of each month across years. The global-mean SST anomaly was then computed on the native grid—area-weighted with each model’s `areacello` for CMIP6, and with cos-latitude weights for ERSSTv5—and subtracted from every native grid cell. CESM2’s `areacello` was additionally masked where `sftof > 0`, and all model `areacello` fields were masked where the first `tos` time step was missing. No observed climatology was applied to models, and near-freezing `tos` values were not treated as missing.

The anomaly fields were then regridded to a common 1° cell-centre grid over the PDO domain: latitude **20.5–69.5°N** and longitude **110.5–259.5°E** (representing 20–70°N, 110–260°E). Regular grids were interpolated with xarray `interp(..., method="linear")`; curvilinear grids used SciPy `LinearNDInterpolator`. A 0/1 ocean mask was interpolated and applied, and cells with any missing monthly value over 1950–2014 were excluded from the EOF. The leading EOF used area weights `sqrt(cos(latitude))`; the leading PC was standardized to zero mean and unit variance. The sign was fixed so that a positive index corresponds to negative SST anomalies in the central/western North Pacific, tested over 30–45°N, 140–180°E. The regression pattern is the time-mean product of grid-cell anomalies with the standardized PC, in °C per standard deviation. Amplitude is the cos-latitude-weighted RMS of that regression pattern. The 12-month lag autocorrelation is the Pearson correlation between the standardized index and itself shifted by 12 months.

The ERSSTv5-derived standardized index versus the published PDO monthly series over 1950–2014 has Pearson **r = 0.958**. The code’s effective-sample-size correction used N = 780, lag-1 monthly autocorrelations r1 = 0.881 for the ERSST-derived index and r2 = 0.861 for the published PDO, giving N_eff = 107.281 and p ≈ 8.64 × 10⁻⁵⁹. This demonstrates temporal agreement in phase and variability between the ERSSTv5-derived leading mode and the published PDO over this window. It does **not** prove that the computed EOF is uniquely the PDO, does not validate CMIP6 models, and does not establish causality. No detrending was applied, so shared trend remains part of the agreement. Pattern-correlation p-values were not used; only the coefficients and shared-cell counts were reported.

**Observed phase transitions.** From the ERSSTv5 standardized index, the 108-month centred running mean changed sign at raw crossings 1961, 1962, 1976, and 2002. Merging crossings less than 3 years after the last counted transition produced **1961, 1976, 2002**. Comparing these with the code’s documented Pacific-shift list **1976, 1989, 1998** within ±2 years, only **1976** matched; 1961 and 2002 did not match entries in that list. Transition years were defined only from ERSSTv5; CMIP6 historical runs are single-member and were not evaluated on individual-year event timing.

**Model assessment.** CESM2 ranks first on spatial pattern correlation and is closest to the observed explained variance (26.1% vs 24.7%) and amplitude (0.363 vs 0.335 °C std⁻¹), but its lag-12 autocorrelation (0.224) is much lower than observed (0.351). GFDL-ESM4 has the second-best pattern correlation, lower amplitude (0.293), much lower explained variance (14.8%), and persistence closest to observed (0.297). MPI-ESM1-2-LR has the weakest pattern correlation, the highest amplitude (0.407) and the highest persistence (0.424). Thus the models capture the broad spatial pattern to differing degrees, but their amplitude and persistence statistics are not uniformly consistent with ERSSTv5; CESM2 is best for pattern and amplitude, while GFDL-ESM4 is closest for persistence.
