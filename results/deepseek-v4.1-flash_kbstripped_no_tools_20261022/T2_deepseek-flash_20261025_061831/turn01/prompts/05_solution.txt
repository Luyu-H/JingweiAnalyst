Rebuild the Nino 3.4 index from the raw ERSSTv5 sea surface temperature field and check it against the series NOAA/CPC publishes, then use the same code to reproduce the offset between CPC's two baseline periods.

- The CPC monthly Nino index on the 1991-2020 baseline (`ersst5_91-20`), Nino 3.4 anomaly column: the main reference.
- The CPC monthly Nino index on the 1981-2010 baseline (`ersst5_81-10`), Nino 3.4 anomaly column: the control.
- The CPC ONI series (`oni`), the official 3-month running index.

Expected numerical results:

Against the CPC 1991-2020 Nino 3.4 anomaly over that window, report the Pearson correlation to 6 decimal places, the RMSE in degrees Celsius to 4 decimal places, and the largest absolute deviation in degrees Celsius to 4 decimal places with the year and month at which it occurs.
- The number of El Nino events in RECON_9120 over 1950-2023 under the operational ONI criterion (at least 5 consecutive overlapping 3-month seasons, i.e. values of the 3-month centred running mean, at or above +0.5 degrees Celsius), and the three events with the highest peak values, each with the year and month of its peak and its peak value in degrees Celsius to 2 decimal places.
- The same count and the same three largest peaks for the CPC ONI series under the identical criterion, with an explanation of why the two counts differ.
- The mean baseline offset of the reconstruction, RECON_8110 (the same index relative to the 1981-2010 baseline) minus RECON_9120, as the unweighted mean over the months of 1950-01 to 2020-12, in degrees Celsius to 3 decimal places.
- The mean published baseline offset, CPC 1981-2010 minus CPC 1991-2020 Nino 3.4 anomaly, as the unweighted mean over the same months, in degrees Celsius to 3 decimal places, and how closely it agrees with the reconstructed offset.

The analysis plan:
1. Read the raw ERSSTv5 monthly files with xarray (variable `sst`, native 2° lat/lon, surface only) and subset time to 1950-01 … 2023-12; convert longitudes to the file's own convention so the Niño 3.4 box 5°S–5°N, 170°W–120°W maps onto its grid. In parallel read the CPC index files for products `ersst5_91-20`, `ersst5_81-10` (the Niño 3.4 anomaly column of each) and `oni` (the published ONI values), aligning all four series on a common monthly time axis with pandas, and record the exact variable names/precision each file uses.

2. Build the Niño 3.4 region average of the ERSSTv5 SST field for every month: mask the field on its own 2° grid to 5°S–5°N, 170°W–120°W, then take an **area-weighted** mean over the cells inside the mask that hold valid data (weights from cos(latitude) of the native cells, restricted to valid cells), never a plain mean over lat/lon.
   - Note [omip-weighted-means-and-integrals]: on a regular grid a plain `mean()` over latitude is wrong because cells shrink toward the poles — use the area-weighted-mean operation (`compute_basic_stat`, `op='area_weighted_mean'`) with the mask applied on the field's own grid and weights restricted to valid data. The same applies to every map statistic; `compute_difference_metrics` weights by area by default and `analyze_correlation` needs `weighting='area'` — but the correlation/RMSE/offsets in steps 5, 8 and 9 are over months (one value per month), not over a map, so they stay unweighted over time.

3. From that monthly region-average SST series compute, for each calendar month separately, its climatology over the 1991-2020 period, and subtract the matching climatological month from every month of 1950-01 … 2023-12 to obtain RECON_9120 (this removes the seasonal cycle). Store the 1991-2020 climatology and `base_period='1991-2020'` in the result attributes.
   - Note [anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices]: an anomaly must be built by removing the field's **own** monthly climatology of the chosen period (each calendar month matched to its own climatological month), and the climatology and `base_period` must be recorded in the result attrs — an anomaly is defined only relative to the period its climatology came from.

4. Repeat step 3 with the 1981-2010 monthly climatology to obtain RECON_8110 (attributes `base_period='1981-2010'`), on the same months, so the two reconstructions differ only by the baseline.

5. Compare RECON_9120 with the CPC `ersst5_91-20` Niño 3.4 anomaly over 1950-01 … 2023-12: Pearson correlation (report to 6 dp), RMSE in °C (4 dp), and the largest absolute deviation in °C (4 dp) together with the year and month in which it occurs; keep all intermediate values unrounded.

6. Compute the 3-month centred running mean of RECON_9120 (window centred on each month, so 1950-01 and 2023-12 have no running-mean value) and detect events on that running-mean series with threshold +0.5 °C and a minimum duration of 5 consecutive running-mean values; also detect La Niña events with threshold −0.5 °C and the same minimum duration for the shading in step 9. Count the El Niño events over 1950-2023 and list the three with the highest peak running-mean value, reporting for each the peak value (°C, 2 dp) and the year and month of the centre month of the peak window.
   - Note [anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices]: events are defined operationally by persistence, not by eye — a warm/cold phase counts only if the series stays beyond the threshold for at least `min_duration`=5 consecutive steps (`detect_threshold_events`); a single step beyond the threshold is noise. State which climatology/baseline each series being compared was built on (RECON_9120 on 1991-2020, CPC `ersst5_91-20` on 1991-2020, CPC `ersst5_81-10` on 1981-2010, `oni` on its own published base period), since CPC products with different baselines are not interchangeable.

7. Apply the identical criterion of step 6 to the published CPC `oni` series directly (ONI is already the 3-month running index, so no additional smoothing): count El Niño events over its available record and report the three highest peaks with year, month and peak value (°C, 2 dp). Then report why the two counts differ, comparing the baselines the two series rest on (step 6 note), the precision at which the CPC series is distributed versus the raw ERSST-derived values, and the running-mean months lost at the ends of the record (1950-01 and 2023-12 for RECON, the ONI product's own first/last months), which truncate events that straddle a record edge.

8. Compute the reconstructed baseline offset: RECON_8110 minus RECON_9120, month by month, then the **unweighted** mean over the months 1950-01 … 2020-12 (°C, 3 dp). Do the same with the published series (CPC `ersst5_81-10` Niño 3.4 anomaly minus CPC `ersst5_91-20` Niño 3.4 anomaly) over the same months (°C, 3 dp), and quantify the agreement with the reconstructed offset (their difference, °C, at the same precision).

9. Figure 1: two rows sharing a 1950-2023 time axis. Top row: RECON_9120 and CPC `ersst5_91-20` monthly anomalies overlaid, the 3-month running mean of RECON_9120, the +0.5 and −0.5 °C threshold lines, and shading between the threshold and the running-mean curve — warm where a detected El Niño event (step 6) is active, cool where a detected La Niña event is active. Bottom row: RECON_9120 minus CPC on its own vertical scale. Both vertical axes labelled in °C.

10. Figure 2: square scatter of RECON_9120 (y) against CPC `ersst5_91-20` (x), one point per month of 1950-2023, identical axis ranges and tick spacing on both axes, a 1:1 reference line, and the step-5 correlation and RMSE annotated on the panel.

Other notes:
- [anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices] RECON_9120 and CPC `ersst5_91-20` share the 1991-2020 baseline, whereas RECON_8110 / CPC `ersst5_81-10` are on 1981-2010; two series on different baselines differ by one constant per calendar month, so the offsets in step 8 are a baseline definition, not an error.

- All values are carried unrounded through the steps and rounded only where reported (6 dp for r, 4 dp for RMSE/max deviation, 2 dp for peak values, 3 dp for the offsets).

def read_nino_monthly(path):
    """Read one CPC monthly Nino file and return the Nino3.4 anomaly series."""
    rows = []
    with open(path, "r") as f:
        for line in f:
            parts = line.strip().split()
            if len(parts) < 10:
                continue
            try:
                yr = int(float(parts[0]))
                mon = int(float(parts[1]))
            except (ValueError, TypeError):
                continue
            vals = []
            for p in parts[:10]:
                try:
                    vals.append(float(p))
                except ValueError:
                    vals.append(np.nan)
            rows.append(vals)

    df = pd.DataFrame(
        rows,
        columns=[
            "YR", "MON",
            "NINO1+2", "ANOM1",
            "NINO3", "ANOM2",
            "NINO4", "ANOM3",
            "NINO3.4", "ANOM4",
        ],
    )
    # The raw files use a large negative missing value; anomalies are small.
    df = df.mask(df < -90.0).dropna(subset=["YR", "MON"])
    df["time"] = pd.to_datetime(
        dict(year=df["YR"].astype(int), month=df["MON"].astype(int), day=1)
    )
    s = df.set_index("time")["ANOM4"].sort_index()
    s.name = "NINO3.4_ANOM"
    s.attrs["units"] = "degC"
    return s


def read_oni(path):
    """Read CPC seasonal ONI and map each 3-month season to its centre month."""
    season_to_month = {
        "DJF": 1, "JFM": 2, "FMA": 3, "MAM": 4, "AMJ": 5, "MJJ": 6,
        "JJA": 7, "JAS": 8, "ASO": 9, "SON": 10, "OND": 11, "NDJ": 12,
    }
    rows = []
    with open(path, "r") as f:
        for line in f:
            parts = line.strip().split()
            if len(parts) < 4:
                continue
            seas = parts[0].upper()
            if seas not in season_to_month:
                continue
            try:
                yr = int(float(parts[1]))
                total = float(parts[2])
                anom = float(parts[3])
            except (ValueError, TypeError):
                continue
            if yr < 0:
                continue
            if anom < -90.0:
                anom = np.nan
            rows.append((seas, yr, total, anom))

    df = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["SEAS", "YR", "TOTAL", "ANOM"])
    df["month"] = df["SEAS"].map(season_to_month)
    df["time"] = pd.to_datetime(
        dict(year=df["YR"].astype(int), month=df["month"].astype(int), day=1)
    )
    s = df.set_index("time")["ANOM"].sort_index()
    s.name = "ONI_ANOM"
    s.attrs["units"] = "degC"
    return s


def detect_threshold_events(series, threshold, min_duration=5, direction="above"):
    """Find contiguous threshold exceedances of at least min_duration points."""
    s = pd.Series(series).sort_index()
    if direction == "above":
        mask = s.notna() & (s >= threshold)
    elif direction == "below":
        mask = s.notna() & (s <= threshold)
    else:
        raise ValueError("direction must be 'above' or 'below'")

    m = mask.to_numpy()
    times = s.index
    n = len(s)
    events = []
    i = 0
    while i < n:
        if m[i]:
            start = i
            while i + 1 < n and m[i + 1]:
                i += 1
            end = i
            length = end - start + 1
            if length >= min_duration:
                seg = s.iloc[start:end + 1]
                if direction == "above":
                    peak_time = seg.idxmax()
                    peak_value = float(seg.max())
                else:
                    peak_time = seg.idxmin()
                    peak_value = float(seg.min())
                events.append({
                    "start": times[start],
                    "end": times[end],
                    "length": int(length),
                    "peak_time": peak_time,
                    "peak_value": peak_value,
                })
        i += 1
    return events


def events_to_mask(series, events):
    mask = pd.Series(False, index=series.index)
    for e in events:
        mask.loc[e["start"]:e["end"]] = True
    return mask

def build_recon_anomaly(series, base_start, base_end, base_label, name):
    s = series.copy().sort_index()
    base = s.loc[base_start:base_end]
    clim = base.groupby(base.index.month).mean()
    anom_values = s.to_numpy() - clim.reindex(s.index.month).to_numpy()
    anom = pd.Series(anom_values, index=s.index, name=name)
    anom.attrs["base_period"] = base_label
    anom.attrs["climatology"] = clim.to_dict()
    anom.attrs["units"] = "degC"
    anom.attrs["long_name"] = "Nino 3.4 SST anomaly reconstructed from ERSSTv5"
    return anom, clim

## 2. Methodology (as actually executed)

**Data and grid.** ERSSTv5 was opened with xarray from `sst.mnmean.nc` (variables `sst`, `time_bnds`), and `sst` was subset to 1950-01-01 … 2023-12-31, giving 888 monthly fields on the native grid: 89 latitudes (−88° to 88°, 2°) × 180 longitudes (0°–358°, 2°). Longitudes in the file already run 0–360; the code applied `lon % 360` before masking, so no re-ordering was needed.

**Region average (spatially area-weighted).** A boolean mask was built on the file's own grid: `lat >= -5 & lat <= 5` (which selects the five cell rows at −4°, −2°, 0°, 2°, 4°) and `lon%360 >= 190 & lon%360 <= 240` (26 cell columns covering 170°W–120°W). The region mean for each month was formed as

`Σ(sst_box · cos(lat)) / Σ(valid · cos(lat))`

i.e. a cosine-latitude area-weighted mean, with the sums taken over both `lat` and `lon` and restricted to valid (non-missing) cells via the `valid` indicator — not a plain latitude or grid-cell mean. The denominator is constant at 129.84 (= Σcos over the 5 latitudes × 26 longitudes) for all 888 months, confirming no missing data inside the box; all 130 cells were used every month.

**Anomalies and baselines.** For each calendar month separately, a climatology was computed as the mean of that month's region-mean SST over the baseline years, then subtracted from every month, month-matched:
- 1991-01 … 2020-12 climatology → **RECON_9120** (888 months, 1950-01 … 2023-12); the climatology and `base_period='1991-2020'` were stored in the result `attrs`.
- 1981-01 … 2010-12 climatology → **RECON_8110**, same months, differing from RECON_9120 only through the baseline.

**CPC series.** The CPC ASCII files were parsed column-wise; the Niño 3.4 anomaly is the 10th token (`ANOM4`), with values below −90 treated as missing. `ersst5_91-20` (1991-2020 base), `ersst5_81-10` (1981-2010 base) and the seasonal `oni` file (4th column, anomaly) were read; the ONI 3-letter seasons were mapped to their **centre month** (DJF→Jan, … NDJ→Dec) to put them on the same monthly axis. Record lengths found: CPC 91-20 = 918 months, CPC 81-10 = 852 months (ends Dec 2020), ONI = 919 months.

**Statistics over time (unweighted).** All step-5, step-8 and step-9 statistics are computed across months (one value per month), not across the map, so no spatial weighting enters them. Correlation is the Pearson product-moment correlation of the paired monthly series; RMSE is `sqrt(mean((recon − cpc)²))`; the largest absolute deviation is `max|recon − cpc|` with its timestamp.

**Event detection.** A **3-month centred running mean** (`rolling(window=3, center=True, min_periods=3)`) was applied to RECON_9120, so 1950-01 and 2023-12 have no running-mean value. Events were then found as **contiguous runs of at least 5 consecutive running-mean values** at or above +0.5 °C (El Niño) or at or below −0.5 °C (La Niña); the event peak is the extremum within the run and its date is the centre month of the peak 3-month window.
