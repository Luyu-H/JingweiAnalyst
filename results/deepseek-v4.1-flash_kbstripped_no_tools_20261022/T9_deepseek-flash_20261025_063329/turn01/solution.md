The analysis succeeded. The PDO was reconstructed for ERSSTv5 and three CMIP6 historical `tos` simulations by removing each source’s own 1950–2014 monthly climatology, removing the area-weighted global-mean SST anomaly, linearly interpolating to the common 1° grid over 20°N–70°N, 110°E–260°E, applying one shared finite-data ocean mask, and taking the leading EOF of the masked anomalies after sqrt(cos-latitude) weighting. The leading PC was standardized over 1950–2014, its sign fixed so that positive index corresponds to negative central/western North Pacific SST anomalies, and the regression pattern was computed as anomalies regressed on the standardized index. Amplitude is the area-weighted RMS of that regression pattern in °C per SD. The lag-1-year autocorrelation is the Pearson correlation of the standardized index with itself at a 12-month lag. Spatial pattern correlations are plain unweighted Pearson correlations over the common valid cells.

**Key results (1950–2014, common 1° North Pacific grid; 4,339 shared ocean cells):**

| Source | Variance explained | Amplitude (°C/SD) | Lag-12-month autocorr. | Pattern correlation with ERSSTv5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ERSSTv5 | 24.6% | 0.334 | 0.355 | 1.000 |
| CESM2 | 26.4% | 0.362 | 0.233 | 0.932 |
| GFDL-ESM4 | 15.6% | 0.291 | 0.285 | 0.897 |

The ERSSTv5-derived standardized index correlates **0.958** with the published CPC PDO series over 1950–2014 when compared as-is, without detrending. This demonstrates temporal agreement between the reconstructed leading-mode index and the published PDO series. It does not demonstrate identical spatial patterns, a causal mechanism, or independent model validation. The published PDO file parsed by the code did not contain a line with “base”; therefore the recorded `base_period` was `None`. The reconstructed indices use the 1950–2014 baseline, and the published PDO was standardized over the same window only for plotting.

**Model assessment:** CESM2 reproduces the observed spatial pattern best, with a plain spatial pattern correlation of **0.932**. GFDL-ESM4 is second at **0.897**, while MPI-ESM1-2-LR is lowest at **0.788**. CESM2 also has the closest amplitude to ERSSTv5 (0.362 vs. 0.334 °C/SD), although its 12-month persistence is weaker (0.233 vs. 0.355). GFDL-ESM4 underestimates both amplitude (0.291) and persistence (0.285) and has the lowest explained variance (15.6%). MPI-ESM1-2-LR has the closest persistence to observations (0.409 vs. 0.355) but overestimates amplitude (0.399) and has the weakest pattern match. Thus the models are not uniformly consistent with the observed PDO: CESM2 is best for pattern and amplitude, MPI is closest for persistence but too strong and spatially less accurate, and GFDL is relatively weak in both amplitude and variance.

**Observed phase transitions:** The 108-month centred running mean was valid only where a full 108-month window existed, so the smoothed record effectively spans centres from 1954-07 to 2010-07. The detected raw sign changes were 1961, 1962, 1976 and 2002; the 1962 crossing was merged into 1961 because it was less than 3 years after the previous counted transition. The resulting transition years are **1961, 1976 and 2002**. Against the documented shift years used in the code comparison set (1957, 1976, 1988, 1998, with ±1-year tolerance), only **1976** corresponds to a documented Pacific climate shift, i.e. the 1976/77 regime shift. The 1961 and 2002 transitions did not match that comparison set.

One methodological caveat from the actual code: the common-grid mapping used `xarray` linear interpolation for ERSSTv5 and GFDL-ESM4, and SciPy `LinearNDInterpolator` for the curvilinear CESM2 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR native grids.
