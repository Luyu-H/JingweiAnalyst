# Plan

1. Load CMIP6 historical `no3` and `po4` monthly for 1971-01 to 2000-12 for CESM2, GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR with `load_regular_data`; load each model's own `volcello` with `load_volumes_for_cmip6`; load each model's depth levels and layer boundaries with `load_depth_levels`. Load WOA23 annual `nitrate` and `phosphate` with `load_climatology_data`; load the WOA23 `basinmask_01` with `load_basin_mask`; load WOA23 cell areas with `load_areas` and depth levels/boundaries with `load_depth_levels`.
2. Convert CMIP6 `no3` and `po4` to micromoles per kilogram using the stated constant density of 1025 kg m-3, using `transform_unit` for unit handling and the stated density conversion where needed. Keep WOA23 nitrate and phosphate in micromoles per kilogram.
3. For each model, compute the 1971-2000 climatology of `no3` and `po4` with `temporal_aggregate` as a day-weighted long-term mean; use the WOA23 annual climatology directly. Restrict all fields to the 200-1000 m layer using each source's depth levels and boundaries from `load_depth_levels`. Use each model's `volcello` and WOA23 cell area multiplied by layer thickness as the volume weights through `compute_basic_stat`.
4. For each of the four sources, compute cell-by-cell N:P with `compute_ratio` at every valid 200-1000 m cell where both nitrate and phosphate are present and phosphate is greater than zero. Compute the headline mean N:P as the volume-weighted mean of the cell-by-cell N:P ratio with `compute_basic_stat`, reported to 2 decimal places. Separately compute the volume-weighted mean nitrate and mean phosphate with `compute_basic_stat`, take their ratio with `compute_ratio` or arithmetic, and report that ratio-of-means N:P to 2 decimal places, clearly labelled.
5. For each source, fit the unweighted ordinary least-squares regression NO3 = a * PO4 + b over all valid 200-1000 m cells with `fit_multivariate_relation`, with each cell weighted equally. Report slope a to 2 decimal places, intercept b in micromoles per kilogram to 2 decimal places, and R-squared to 3 decimal places.
6. For each source, use `compute_exceedance_fraction` by volume on the 200-1000 m N:P field to compute the percentage of water volume with N:P strictly between 14 and 18, using the volume fraction greater than 14 minus the volume fraction greater than or equal to 18, reported to 1 decimal place. Use `compute_exceedance_fraction` by volume again to compute the global percentage of water volume with N:P below 12, reported to 1 decimal place.
7. Regrid the WOA23 `basinmask_01` to each model's native horizontal grid with `regrid_horizontal`; keep the WOA23 mask on its native grid for WOA23. Use `select_basin` to isolate the Pacific, Atlantic and Indian basins according to the WOA23 mask, with marginal seas folded into their parent ocean and the Southern and Arctic Oceans excluded. For each source and each of these three basins, compute the volume-weighted headline mean N:P with `compute_basic_stat` and report to 2 decimal places. Also use `compute_exceedance_fraction` by volume to compute the percentage of water volume with N:P below 12 within the Pacific and within the Indian Ocean, reported to 1 decimal place.
8. Figure 1: make a 4-panel scatter plot of nitrate on the y-axis against phosphate on the x-axis in micromoles per kilogram over 200-1000 m, one panel per source in the order WOA23, CESM2, GFDL-ESM4, MPI-ESM1-2-LR. Use identical axis ranges and ticks across panels. In each panel show that source's fitted regression line and the Redfield 16:1 line through the origin, drawn so they are distinguishable, and annotate the panel with a, b and R-squared.
9. Figure 2: for each source, compute the 200-1000 m mean N:P per horizontal cell with `compute_basic_stat`, subtract 16, and produce a 2-by-2 panel of true geographic global maps. Put curvilinear model fields on a regular longitude-latitude grid with `regrid_horizontal` for display only, use a common longitude-latitude frame, and use one shared diverging colour scale centred on zero.
10. Compare each source's headline N:P and basin-mean N:P with Redfield 16:1, and compare the low-N:P volume percentage and its Pacific/Indian basin distribution among the models and WOA23 to assess whether each model reproduces the observed low-N:P denitrification signal.
Other notes:
- WOA23 nitrate and phosphate are an all-period nutrient climatology, while the models are 1971-2000 climatologies, so the comparison is not exactly synchronous.
- The headline N:P is the volume-weighted mean of cell-by-cell N:P ratios; the ratio of mean nitrate to mean phosphate is reported separately and can differ.
- The regression is unweighted, with each valid 3D cell weighted equally, and uses only cells where both nitrate and phosphate are valid.
- N:P ratios require phosphate greater than zero; this is a mathematical requirement of the ratio.
- CMIP6 concentrations are converted to micromoles per kilogram using constant density 1025 kg m-3; WOA23 concentrations are already in micromoles per kilogram.
- Volume percentages use each model's own `volcello` and WOA23 cell area multiplied by layer thickness.
- Global percentages include the Southern and Arctic Oceans; basin-specific means and percentages exclude them.
- The WOA23 basin mask is regridded to each model's native grid for basin statistics; model fields are regridded to a regular longitude-latitude grid only for Figure 2 display.
- The 14-18 percentage is the strict volume fraction between 14 and 18; the below-12 percentage is a separate one-sided fraction.
- Model reproduction is assessed by comparing the reported low-N:P volume fractions and basin means with WOA23.
