# Plan

1. Load ERSSTv5 monthly `sst` for 1950-01 to 2023-12 over 5S-5N, 170W-120W on its native 2-degree grid with `load_regular_data`. In the same step, load the CPC monthly index products with `load_regular_data`: `ersst5_91-20` and `ersst5_81-10` selecting the Nino 3.4 anomaly column, and `oni` selecting the ONI series, for 1950-01 to 2023-12.
2. Build RECON_9120 using the same reconstruction code: apply `compute_anomaly_vs_own_climatology` to the ERSSTv5 Nino 3.4 region field with the reference monthly climatology set to 1991-2020, then reduce over latitude and longitude with `compute_basic_stat` using its area-weighted mean to produce the monthly Nino 3.4 anomaly series for 1950-01 to 2023-12.
3. Build RECON_8110 with the identical code, changing only the reference monthly climatology to 1981-2010 using `compute_anomaly_vs_own_climatology`, followed by the same area-weighted spatial mean with `compute_basic_stat`.
4. Compare RECON_9120 with the CPC `ersst5_91-20` Nino 3.4 anomaly over 1950-01 to 2023-12 using `analyze_correlation` for the Pearson correlation to 6 decimal places, and `compute_difference_metrics` for the RMSE to 4 decimal places, the largest absolute deviation to 4 decimal places, and the year and month of that largest deviation.
5. Compute the 3-month centred running mean of RECON_9120 with `temporal_aggregate` using a 3-month running window. Apply `detect_threshold_events` to this running-mean series with threshold +0.5 degrees Celsius and a minimum duration of 5 consecutive overlapping 3-month seasons to count El Nino events over 1950-2023 and extract each episode’s peak timing and peak value. Rank the detected events by peak value and report the three highest peaks with year, month, and peak value to 2 decimal places. Also detect La Nina events at -0.5 degrees Celsius with the same 5-season minimum duration for the shading in Figure 1.
6. Apply `detect_threshold_events` to the CPC `oni` series directly, since ONI is already the official 3-month running index, using the same +0.5 degrees Celsius threshold and 5-consecutive-season minimum duration. Count the El Nino events and report the three highest peaks with year, month, and peak value to 2 decimal places; compare this count with the RECON_9120 count.
7. For the baseline offsets over 1950-01 to 2020-12, use `compute_difference_metrics` on RECON_8110 minus RECON_9120 to obtain the unweighted mean monthly baseline offset to 3 decimal places. Use `compute_difference_metrics` again on the CPC `ersst5_81-10` Nino 3.4 anomaly minus the CPC `ersst5_91-20` Nino 3.4 anomaly over the same months to obtain the published mean baseline offset to 3 decimal places. Compare the two offsets, for example by their difference, and state how closely they agree.
8. Produce Figure 1 with custom plotting code from the monthly series and detected events: a two-row figure sharing a 1950-2023 time axis; the upper row overlays RECON_9120 and CPC 1991-2020 monthly anomalies, the 3-month running mean, the +0.5 and -0.5 degrees Celsius thresholds, and shading between the threshold and running-mean curve, warm during detected El Nino events and cool during detected La Nina events; the lower row shows RECON_9120 minus CPC on its own vertical scale; label both vertical axes in degrees Celsius.
9. Produce Figure 2 with custom plotting code: a square scatter plot of RECON_9120 on the y-axis against the CPC 1991-2020 series on the x-axis, one point per month, with identical axis ranges and ticks, a 1:1 reference line, and the correlation and RMSE annotated.

Other notes:
- RECON_9120 is the area-weighted Nino 3.4 SST anomaly over 5S-5N, 170W-120W relative to a 1991-2020 monthly climatology; RECON_8110 is the same index relative to 1981-2010.
- Event counting uses the 3-month centred running mean and requires at least five consecutive overlapping 3-month seasons at or above +0.5 degrees Celsius for El Nino, or at or below -0.5 degrees Celsius for La Nina.
- The RECON and CPC/ONI event counts can differ because CPC’s published index uses its own product version, quality control, baseline, and spatial averaging; small discrepancies near the threshold or near a five-season duration can change whether a run is classified as an event.
- Baseline offsets are unweighted means over the months 1950-01 to 2020-12 only.
- The largest-deviation location is reported as the year and month of occurrence.
- Figures require custom plotting code because no plotting tool is listed.
