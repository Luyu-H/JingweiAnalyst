# Plan

1. Load GLODAPv2.2016b climatology from `glodap` for `TCO2`, `TAlk` and pH (using the total-scale in-situ-temperature pH field, `pHtsinsitutp`) with `load_climatology_data`; load its depth levels with `load_depth_levels` and its 1-degree cell areas with `load_areas`.
2. Load `cmip6_historical` monthly fields for CESM2, GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR: for 2002 `dissic`, `talk`, `ph`, `thetao` and `so`; for 1971-2000 `dissic`, `thetao` and `so`; load each model's own `areacello` and `volcello` using `load_regular_data` and `load_volumes_for_cmip6`.
3. For each model, compute potential density anomaly from `thetao` and `so` with `compute_potential_density_anomaly` (TEOS-10), add 1000 kg m-3 to obtain density, and convert monthly `dissic` and `talk` from mol m-3 to micromoles per kilogram using that density; use `transform_unit` for the pure unit scaling where applicable. Keep `ph` on its native total scale.
4. Use `temporal_aggregate` to form each model's 2002 annual mean for `dissic`, `talk`, `ph` (and the density-converted DIC/talk fields), and each model's 1971-2000 long-term mean for `dissic`.
5. For the 0-50 m global means, select each product's own depth levels within 0-50 m using `load_depth_levels`, then use `compute_basic_stat` with model `volcello`/`areacello` and GLODAP spatial weights to compute one global mean per source for DIC, total alkalinity and pH. Subtract GLODAP from each model to obtain the 0-50 m differences; report DIC and total alkalinity in micromoles per kilogram and pH to 3 decimal places.
6. For the 0-50 m DIC maps, use `compute_basic_stat` to average each cell over its own 0-50 m levels for each model and for GLODAP; regrid each model field to the GLODAP 1-degree grid with `regrid_horizontal`; use `build_comparison_mask` for each model-GLODAP pair and then `compute_difference_metrics` for mean bias and RMSE and `analyze_correlation` for spatial correlation on the common cells.
7. For the vertical DIC bias profile, use `regrid_vertical` to put each model's DIC on the GLODAP depth levels and `regrid_horizontal` to the GLODAP 1-degree grid; at 0, 100, 500, 1000 and 2000 m use `build_comparison_mask` for model-GLODAP common cells at that depth and `compute_difference_metrics` or `compute_basic_stat` to average model minus GLODAP over those cells with the appropriate spatial weights.
8. Compute the 2002 minus 1971-2000 change in global 0-50 m mean DIC for each model using the `temporal_aggregate` results and `compute_basic_stat`; compute its ratio to that model's first-item 0-50 m DIC difference from GLODAP using `compute_ratio`.
9. Compare the models using the global-mean biases for DIC, total alkalinity and pH, the map bias/RMSE/correlation, and the vertical DIC biases; judge which model best reproduces GLODAP and whether the DIC, total alkalinity and pH errors are consistent in sign.
10. Create Figure 1: three global maps in one row, one per model, of 0-50 m DIC bias against GLODAP on the common GLODAP grid, sharing one diverging colour scale centred on zero and labelled in micromoles per kilogram.
11. Create Figure 2: a single panel of the vertical profile of global mean DIC bias, with depth increasing downward, bias in micromoles per kilogram on the horizontal axis, one line per model, a vertical zero reference line, and the five sampled depths marked.
Other notes:
- Model DIC and total alkalinity are converted to micromoles per kilogram using TEOS-10 density from each model's `thetao` and `so`; GLODAP `TCO2` and `TAlk` are already in micromoles per kilogram.
- GLODAP pH is taken from the total-scale in-situ-temperature field `pHtsinsitutp`; model `ph` is used as reported on the total scale.
- The 0-50 m statistics use each product's own depth levels within 0-50 m, as requested; map and profile comparisons use the GLODAP 1-degree grid and only cells defined in both fields.
- GLODAP is a 1972-2013 climatology, so comparison with model year 2002 includes temporal representativeness differences.
- The ratio of the 2002-minus-1971/2000 DIC change to the GLODAP DIC bias indicates how much of the apparent bias could reflect model drift or period choice rather than a pure model error; a ratio near one means the bias is comparable to the model's own historical change.
