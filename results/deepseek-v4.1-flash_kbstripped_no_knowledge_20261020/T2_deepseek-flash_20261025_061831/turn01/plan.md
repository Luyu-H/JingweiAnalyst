# Plan

1. Load data: use `load_regular_data` on `ersst` for monthly `sst` over 1950-01 to 2023-12 in the box 5°S–5°N, 170°W–120°W on the native 2° grid. Use `load_regular_data` on `cpc_indices` for product `ersst5_91-20` (Nino 3.4 anomaly), product `ersst5_81-10` (Nino 3.4 anomaly), and product `oni` (ONI series), over the same period/available months; align all series on common monthly timestamps.
2. Build RECON_9120: use `compute_enso_nino_index` on the loaded ERSSTv5 Nino 3.4 box with the 1991–2020 monthly climatology to produce the area-weighted monthly SST anomaly series for 1950-01–2023-12. Build RECON_8110 with the same call/code, changing only the climatology period to 1981–2010. Keep unrounded values.
3. Compare RECON_9120 with the CPC `ersst5_91-20` Nino 3.4 anomaly over 1950-01–2023-12 using `analyze_correlation` for Pearson correlation (report 6 dp) and `compute_difference_metrics` for RMSE (4 dp) and the largest absolute deviation with its year and month (4 dp).
4. Compute the 3-month centred running mean of RECON_9120 with `temporal_aggregate` (running window). Use `detect_threshold_events` with threshold +0.5°C and minimum duration 5 consecutive overlapping 3-month seasons to identify El Niño episodes; report the count and the three highest peaks, each with peak year-month and peak value (2 dp). Use the same tool with threshold -0.5°C and the same minimum duration on the same RECON running mean to identify La Niña episodes for shading.
5. Apply `detect_threshold_events` directly to the CPC ONI series (already a 3-month running index) with threshold +0.5°C and minimum duration 5 consecutive overlapping seasons; report the count and the three highest peaks with year-month and peak value (2 dp). Compare the counts and explain the discrepancy by the operational ONI’s evolving 30-year base-period convention and official processing/rounding versus the fixed 1991–2020 reconstruction.
6. Compute RECON_8110 minus RECON_9120 month by month; use `compute_basic_stat` to take the unweighted mean over months 1950-01–2020-12 (3 dp). Compute CPC `ersst5_81-10` minus CPC `ersst5_91-20` Nino 3.4 anomaly over the same months with `compute_basic_stat` (3 dp), and compare the two offsets (e.g. their difference/agreement).
7. Plot Figure 1 with plotting code: two rows sharing the 1950–2023 time axis; upper row overlays RECON_9120 and CPC `ersst5_91-20` monthly anomalies, the RECON 3-month running mean, +0.5 and -0.5°C threshold lines, and shading between the threshold and running-mean curve for detected El Niño (warm) and La Niña (cool) episodes; lower row shows RECON_9120 minus CPC on its own vertical scale; both y-axes labelled °C.
8. Plot Figure 2 with plotting code: square scatter of RECON_9120 (y) against CPC `ersst5_91-20` (x), one point per month, identical axis ranges and ticks, 1:1 reference line, and annotation of the correlation and RMSE from step 3.

Other notes:
- Match all series on common monthly timestamps and restrict correlation/RMSE/max-deviation comparison to 1950-01–2023-12; restrict baseline-offset means to 1950-01–2020-12.
- The operational ONI is already a 3-month running mean, whereas RECON_9120 must first be converted to a centred 3-month running mean before applying the identical 5-season threshold rule.
- Event peaks are the maximum 3-month running-mean values within each detected episode, not the raw monthly anomalies.
- The CPC ONI event count can differ from RECON_9120 because ONI uses NOAA’s operational 30-year base periods updated every five years (and official processing/rounding), not one fixed 1991–2020 monthly climatology; events near ±0.5°C can therefore change category.
- Report correlation to 6 dp, RMSE and largest deviation to 4 dp, peak values to 2 dp, and baseline offsets to 3 dp; do not round intermediate values.
