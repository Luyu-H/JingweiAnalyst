I assessed the 200–1000 m layer using the WOA23 annual objectively analysed climatology and 1971–2000 climatological means from CESM2, GFDL-ESM4 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR. CMIP6 concentrations were converted from mol m⁻³ to µmol kg⁻¹ with a constant density of 1025 kg m⁻³. Basin means use the WOA23 basin mask, with marginal seas folded into their parent ocean and the Southern and Arctic Oceans excluded from the Pacific/Atlantic/Indian means.

### Assessment

All four sources sit **below Redfield** on their headline mean N:P. WOA23 has a mean of **14.25** (ratio of means **14.10**), with a relatively tight NO₃–PO₄ relationship (slope **12.79**, intercept **2.67**, R² **0.976**). Its basin structure is Atlantic-highest (**15.65**), then Indian (**14.09**) and Pacific (**13.89**), which is the expected qualitative basin-scale low-N:P signature of denitrification. However, in this 200–1000 m annual layer-mean diagnostic, WOA23 has **no volume with N:P <12** globally, in the Pacific, or in the Indian Ocean. Thus the observed low-N:P signal here is a **relative basin-scale depletion**, especially in the Pacific/Indian, rather than a sub-12 volume in the layer mean.

The models do not faithfully reproduce the observed low-N:P volume:

- **CESM2** is much too low: headline N:P **11.76** (ratio of means **10.91**), with a very shallow OLS slope (**2.42**) and high intercept (**16.03**), and almost no coherent NO₃–PO₄ covariance (R² **0.062**). It produces excessive sub-12 water: **38.0%** globally, **66.5%** in the Pacific, and **32.4%** in the Indian Ocean, far above WOA23. It preserves the qualitative basin ordering Atlantic > Indian > Pacific, but strongly overestimates the low-N:P volume.
- **GFDL-ESM4** is closest overall: headline N:P **13.99** (ratio of means **13.73**), slope **11.17**, intercept **4.31**, R² **0.816**. Its basin ordering matches WOA23 (Atlantic > Indian > Pacific) and it is near the observed global mean. It still produces more sub-12 water than WOA23 (**7.9%** global, **6.1%** Pacific), and its Indian mean is slightly high (**14.62** vs **14.09**), but it is the least biased of the three models.
- **MPI-ESM1-2-LR** is lower than observed: headline N:P **13.09** (ratio of means **12.65**), slope **8.07**, intercept **6.41**, R² **0.660**. It has the correct qualitative basin ordering, but all basin means are low. It overproduces sub-12 water globally (**21.8%**) and in the Pacific (**12.1%**), while having essentially none in the Indian Ocean; only **0.5%** of its volume lies in the 14–18 range.

Overall, the models reproduce the qualitative basin-scale pattern—Atlantic highest, Pacific lowest, with the Pacific/Indian depleted relative to the Atlantic—but they do not reproduce the observed low-N:P volume. They tend to **overproduce sub-12 N:P water**, most severely in CESM2 and moderately in MPI-ESM1-2-LR, while GFDL-ESM4 is closest but still somewhat too low in the Pacific and too broad in the 14–18 range. The WOA23 low-N:P denitrification signal is therefore better represented as a moderate basin-scale Pacific/Indian deficit than as the large sub-12 volumes generated by CESM2 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR.
