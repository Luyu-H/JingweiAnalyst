The CMIP6 historical 2002 annual means were compared with the GLODAPv2.2016b mapped climatology on native model grids and, for the mapped statistics, on the GLODAP 1° grid. DIC and total alkalinity are in µmol kg⁻¹; pH is on the total scale.

### 1. Global 0–50 m means and biases

| Source | DIC mean | DIC bias vs GLODAP | Total alkalinity | TAlk bias vs GLODAP | pH | pH bias vs GLODAP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GLODAPv2.2016b | 2032 | — | 2303 | — | 8.082 | — |
| CESM2 | 1997 | −36 | 2264 | −39 | 8.080 | −0.003 |
| GFDL-ESM4 | 2061 | +29 | 2335 | +32 | 8.087 | +0.005 |
| MPI-ESM1-2-LR | 1974 | −59 | 2217 | −86 | 8.059 | −0.023 |

CESM2 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR are lower than GLODAP in DIC, alkalinity and pH. GFDL-ESM4 is higher than GLODAP in all three. GFDL-ESM4 has the smallest absolute global-mean DIC and alkalinity offsets, while CESM2 has the smallest pH offset.

### 2. 0–50 m DIC map comparison on the GLODAP 1° grid

| Model | Mean bias (µmol kg⁻¹) | RMSE (µmol kg⁻¹) | Spatial correlation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CESM2 | −34 | 61 | 0.812 |
| GFDL-ESM4 | +30 | 75 | 0.660 |
| MPI-ESM1-2-LR | −58 | 89 | 0.665 |

CESM2 clearly has the lowest RMSE and the highest spatial correlation for the 0–50 m DIC pattern. The MPI model has the largest negative DIC bias and largest RMSE.

### 4. 2002 minus 1971–2000 mean 0–50 m DIC change and its ratio to the GLODAP offset

| Model | ΔDIC 2002 − 1971–2000 (µmol kg⁻¹) | 0–50 m DIC bias vs GLODAP | Ratio ΔDIC / bias |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CESM2 | +9 | −36 | −0.26 |
| GFDL-ESM4 | +12 | +29 | +0.43 |
| MPI-ESM1-2-LR | +11 | −59 | −0.18 |

The 31-year DIC increase is small compared with the model–GLODAP offsets. For CESM2 and MPI, the period change is opposite in sign to the DIC bias, so a correction for comparing 2002 with an older climatology would make the negative offsets larger. For GFDL, the positive period change is the same sign as the positive bias and accounts for roughly 43% of the apparent offset, but a substantial positive bias remains. Therefore the DIC offsets should mostly be interpreted as model mean-state or biogeochemical/parameterisation errors, not simply as an artefact of the time-window mismatch.

### 5. Which model performs best, and sign consistency

Overall, **CESM2 reproduces the observed 0–50 m carbon system best**: it has the lowest map RMSE (61 µmol kg⁻¹), the highest spatial correlation (0.812), a small pH bias (−0.003), and moderate DIC and alkalinity biases. GFDL-ESM4 has slightly smaller absolute global-mean DIC and alkalinity biases than CESM2, but its spatial pattern is poorer (RMSE 75 µmol kg⁻¹, correlation 0.660). MPI-ESM1-2-LR performs worst overall, with the largest DIC and alkalinity offsets, the largest pH offset, and the largest map RMSE.

Within each model, the DIC, total-alkalinity and pH biases all have the same sign: negative for CESM2 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR, positive for GFDL-ESM4. Across the three models, however, the sign is not universal: GFDL-ESM4 is the outlier with positive carbon-system biases, whereas CESM2 and MPI-ESM1-2-LR are both biased low.
