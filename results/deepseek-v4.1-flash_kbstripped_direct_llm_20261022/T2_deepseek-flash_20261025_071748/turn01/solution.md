The fixed-base reconstruction reproduces the CPC 1991–2020 monthly Niño 3.4 anomaly almost exactly:

- Pearson correlation: **0.999971** (to 6 d.p.)
- RMSE: **0.0075 °C** (to 4 d.p.)
- Largest absolute deviation: **0.0229 °C** at **1992-01**

Using the operational ONI criterion—at least 5 consecutive overlapping 3-month seasons with the centred 3-month running mean at or above +0.5 °C—the reconstructed series gives:

- **RECON_9120 El Niño events: 16**
- Three highest peaks:
  1. **2015-12, 2.64 °C**
  2. **1997-12, 2.35 °C**
  3. **1983-01, 2.15 °C**

For the published CPC ONI series, applying the identical criterion gives:

- **CPC ONI El Niño events: 22**
- Three highest peaks:
  1. **2015-12, 2.59 °C**
  2. **1997-12, 2.37 °C**
  3. **1983-01, 2.14 °C**

The event counts differ because RECON_9120 uses a fixed 1991–2020 monthly climatology, whereas the official ONI is constructed with the operational 30-year base period updated every five years and is itself a 3-month running mean. The changing base period alters anomalies, especially in the early record, so several weak early El Niño episodes cross the +0.5 °C threshold in the ONI but not in the fixed-base RECON_9120. The nearly perfect monthly agreement with the CPC 1991–2020 index shows that this is a baseline/definition effect, not an error in the Niño 3.4 SST reconstruction.

For the baseline offset over 1950-01 to 2020-12:

- Reconstructed offset, RECON_8110 − RECON_9120: **+0.060 °C**
- Published offset, CPC 1981–2010 − CPC 1991–2020: **+0.058 °C**
- Difference: **+0.002 °C**

Thus the reconstructed baseline offset agrees with the published CPC offset to within **0.002 °C**.
