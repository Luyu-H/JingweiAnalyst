Reconstructed Niño 3.4 from ERSSTv5 as the cosine-latitude-weighted SST anomaly over 5°S–5°N, 170°W–120°W, using a 1991–2020 monthly climatology, for 1950-01 to 2023-12.

Comparison with CPC 1991–2020 monthly Niño 3.4 anomaly:
- Pearson correlation: **0.999972**
- RMSE: **0.0075 °C**
- Largest absolute deviation: **0.0226 °C**, occurring in **1992-01** (January 1992; signed RECON−CPC = **−0.0226 °C**).

El Niño events under the operational ONI criterion (at least five consecutive 3-month centered running-mean values ≥ +0.5 °C):
- **RECON_9120: 16 events**
  - Top three peaks: **2015-12, 2.64 °C**; **1997-12, 2.35 °C**; **1983-01, 2.15 °C**
- **CPC ONI: 22 events**
  - Top three peaks: **2015-12, 2.59 °C**; **1997-12, 2.37 °C**; **1983-01, 2.14 °C**

The count differs by six because the official ONI uses evolving 30-year base periods updated every five years, whereas RECON_9120 uses one fixed 1991–2020 monthly climatology for the whole record. With a warmer modern baseline imposed on early decades, some borderline early El Niño events fall below +0.5 °C in RECON_9120 but qualify in the official ONI. The very small monthly RMSE and near-unity correlation with CPC’s fixed 1991–2020 series indicate that the reconstruction itself is accurate; the event-count difference comes from the baseline definition and operational processing.

Baseline offsets over 1950-01 to 2020-12:
- Reconstructed: **RECON_8110 − RECON_9120 = +0.060 °C**
- Published CPC: **CPC 1981–2010 − CPC 1991–2020 = +0.058 °C**
- Agreement: **0.002 °C**, so the reconstruction reproduces the published baseline offset to within about 0.002 °C.
