- anomaly-baselines-and-climate-indices | method | must | score 8.0 | matched keywords: anomaly, climatology, baseline, index, Nino 3.4, ONI, CPC; datasets: cpc_indices, ersst
  An anomaly from a field's own climatology removes its bias (what an index, a trend or a comparison between two products needs), one from an observed climatology keeps it; an anomaly is defined only relative to its baseline period, so series on different baselines differ by an offset; DJF and JFM seasons must not be mixed; events and outliers are judged on anomalies, with persistence.
- common-comparison-mask | method | must | score 2.0 | matched keywords: RMSE; datasets: ersst
  A bias, RMSE or pattern correlation between two fields exists only where both have data, and several models' cell-by-cell scores can be ranked only on the cells all of them cover; for a cell-by-cell comparison of one field put the fields on one grid, build one mask over all of them and apply it to every field (a pattern that an analysis produced on each source's own domain, such as a variability mode, is correlated with the reference over the cells the two share), and keep WOA23's land-sea mask out of comparisons WOA23 is not part of (GLODAP, for instance).
- cmip6-tos-bulk-top-cell | concept | should | score 1.8 | matched keywords: SST, sea surface temperature; datasets: ersst
  tos is the upper-boundary (top-cell bulk) potential temperature of the liquid ocean, also under sea ice and ice shelves; it is not a skin temperature, and top-cell thickness differs by model.
- eof-modes-and-pdo-index | method | should | score 0.6 | matched datasets: ersst, cpc_indices
  The PDO is the leading EOF of North Pacific SST anomalies with the global-mean anomaly removed; EOFs are area-weighted, their sign is a convention, explained variance depends on grid, domain and record length, and a mode's amplitude is read from its regression pattern on the standardised index, compared between sources with an area-weighted correlation.
- serial-correlation-and-significance | method | should | score 0.6 | matched keywords: correlation
  Monthly ocean series and neighbouring ocean cells are strongly autocorrelated, so naive sample sizes overstate the information: trend intervals and correlation p-values need an effective sample size, a trend question needs an explicit straight-line fit quoted with its interval, shared trends inflate correlations, and fitted relations need spatially blocked validation.
- cf-cell-methods | concept | info | score 0.3 | matched datasets: ersst

===== USER =====
User question: Rebuild the Nino 3.4 index from the raw ERSSTv5 sea surface temperature field and check it against the series NOAA/CPC publishes, then use the same code to reproduce the offset between CPC's two baseline periods.

Data to use:
- ERSSTv5 monthly SST, 1950-01 to 2023-12, on its native 2-degree grid.
- The CPC monthly Nino index on the 1991-2020 baseline (`ersst5_91-20`), Nino 3.4 anomaly column: the main reference.
- The CPC monthly Nino index on the 1981-2010 baseline (`ersst5_81-10`), Nino 3.4 anomaly column: the control.
- The CPC ONI series (`oni`), the official 3-month running index.

Expected numerical results:
- RECON_9120, the monthly Nino 3.4 index reconstructed from ERSSTv5 as the SST anomaly over 5S-5N, 170W-120W relative to a 1991-2020 monthly climatology, 1950-01 to 2023-12. Against the CPC 1991-2020 Nino 3.4 anomaly over that window, report the Pearson correlation to 6 decimal places, the RMSE in degrees Celsius to 4 decimal places, and the largest absolute deviation in degrees Celsius to 4 decimal places with the year and month at which it occurs.
- The number of El Nino events in RECON_9120 over 1950-2023 under the operational ONI criterion (at least 5 consecutive overlapping 3-month seasons, i.e. values of the 3-month centred running mean, at or above +0.5 degrees Celsius), and the three events with the highest peak values, each with the year and month of its peak and its peak value in degrees Celsius to 2 decimal places.
- The same count and the same three largest peaks for the CPC ONI series under the identical criterion, with an explanation of why the two counts differ.
- The mean baseline offset of the reconstruction, RECON_8110 (the same index relative to the 1981-2010 baseline) minus RECON_9120, as the unweighted mean over the months of 1950-01 to 2020-12, in degrees Celsius to 3 decimal places.
- The mean published baseline offset, CPC 1981-2010 minus CPC 1991-2020 Nino 3.4 anomaly, as the unweighted mean over the same months, in degrees Celsius to 3 decimal places, and how closely it agrees with the reconstructed offset.

Expected visualizations:
- Figure 1, a two-row figure sharing a time axis over 1950-2023. The upper row overlays the RECON_9120 and CPC 1991-2020 monthly anomalies, the 3-month running mean, the +0.5 and -0.5 degrees Celsius thresholds, and shading between the threshold and the running-mean curve, warm during detected El Nino events and cool during detected La Nina events (at least 5 consecutive seasons at or below -0.5 degrees Celsius). The lower row shows RECON_9120 minus CPC on its own vertical scale. Label both vertical axes in degrees Celsius.
- Figure 2, a square scatter plot of RECON_9120 (y) against the CPC 1991-2020 series (x), one point per month, with identical axis ranges and ticks, a 1:1 reference line, and the correlation and RMSE annotated.
